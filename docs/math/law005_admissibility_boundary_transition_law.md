# LAW-005: Admissibility Boundary Transition Law

## Candidate Law Statement
### Informal
Projected continuation approaching admissibility boundaries may stabilize, split, collapse, prune, or fail depending on admissibility margin, transport magnitude, and local topology constraints.

### Boundary Definition
$$\partial A_\alpha := \{ z : \text{admissibility\_margin}_A(z) = 0 \}$$

### Stable Projection Condition
If $\text{admissibility\_margin}_A(\Pi_{A_\alpha}(T_\alpha)) > 0$, continuation remains interior-admissible.

### Boundary Transition Condition
If $\text{admissibility\_margin}_A(\Pi_{A_\alpha}(T_\alpha)) \rightarrow 0$, boundary-sensitive continuation modes may activate.

### Projection Failure Condition
If $\Pi_{A_\alpha}(T_\alpha)$ is undefined or leaves $A_\alpha$, continuation may branch-collapse, prune, destabilize, or enter preserved failure states.

### Branch Transition Condition
Boundary proximity may preserve multi-valued continuation through branch splitting or branch pruning rather than forced unique projection.

## Context & Objectives
This law addresses the behavioral outcomes when the aggregate transport vector $T_\alpha$ (defined in LAW-003 and LAW-004) is projected onto the admissibility manifold near its boundaries. It formalizes the transitions between interior stability and boundary-induced structural changes.

The primary objectives are:
- **Boundary Formalization:** Explicitly link the admissibility margin to transition outcomes.
- **Structural Preservation:** Ensure that instabilities, splits, and prunes are treated as valid (governed) process states rather than unhandled errors.
- **Ambiguity Management:** Explicitly allow for branch splitting or multi-valued outcomes at the boundary.
- **Non-Global Stability:** Acknowledge that the framework does not claim universal stability or smoothness at $\partial A$.

## Law Conditions
1. **Boundary definition explicit:** partial A is defined by the zero-margin condition.
2. **Interior admissibility condition explicit:** Criteria for stable continuation are established.
3. **Boundary transition condition explicit:** Behavior as the margin approaches zero is addressed.
4. **Projection failure condition explicit:** Outcomes for undefined or out-of-bounds projections are governed.
5. **Branch splitting allowed:** Multi-valued outcomes can be generated by boundary proximity.
6. **Branch pruning allowed:** Reduction of candidate sets at the boundary is permitted.
7. **Multi-valued continuation preserved:** Rejects forced unique projection at singular boundaries.
8. **No global boundary stability claim:** No assumption of universal boundary smoothness.

## Failure Modes to Preserve
- **Projection Failure:** Valid cases where no admissible candidate can be found via Pi_A.
- **Boundary Instability:** Oscillatory behavior between boundary and interior.
- **Window Collapse:** Singularities where the admissible volume vanishes.
- **Branch Overcollapse:** Incorrectly reducing multiple valid branches to one (or zero).
- **Forced Unique Projection:** Artifacts introduced by assuming unique solutions at singular points.
- **Topology Discontinuity:** Sudden changes in the connected structure of $A$.
- **Transport Induced Boundary Divergence:** Flux-driven escape from the admissible domain.
- **Physics Claim Leakage:** Treating these transitions as verified physical phase changes.
