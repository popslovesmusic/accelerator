# Recursive Boundary Ecology Framework (MPF-RES-008)

## 1. Purpose
This document establishes the **Recursive Boundary Ecology Framework**. Its objective is to model unresolved governance structures as interacting ecological boundary systems. It formalizes containment interaction dynamics, models closure-pressure circulation, and studies stabilization competition within a bounded epistemic environment.

## 2. Core Concepts

### 2.1 Boundary Ecology
A bounded system where unresolved governance structures interact through containment, pressure exchange, and recursive influence. Structures are treated as "governance organisms" rather than isolated entries.

### 2.2 Epistemic Pressure Fields
Distributed governance pressures (e.g., closure pressure, symbolic density) generated by unresolved structures and recursive derivation dynamics.

### 2.3 Containment Symbiosis
Multiple unresolved structures may mutually stabilize one another through bounded interaction without triggering authority escalation or establishing closure.

### 2.4 Recursive Drift Ecology
A network of mutually reinforcing drift behaviors (e.g., semantic inflation, scope bleed) propagating through interaction chains.

## 3. Ecology Classes
- **stabilizing_boundary**: Actively resists drift and maintains local containment.
- **closure_pressure_accumulator**: Collects and concentrates implicit proof expectations.
- **recursive_drift_vector**: Acts as a source or transmitter of epistemic instability.
- **epistemic_sink**: Absorbs and neutralizes symbolic pressure without escalation.

## 4. Interaction Topology
The framework defines how structures interact:
- **recursive_pressure_transfer**: Moving closure pressure between dependency layers.
- **authority_diffusion**: Weakening of central authority through interaction loops.
- **quarantine_absorption**: Incorporating instability into isolated containment fields.

## 5. Protected Boundary Objects
- **LAW034 (Continuation Grammar)**: Restricted from becoming a source of ecological authority or generating recursive globalization pressure. It must remain locally bounded.
