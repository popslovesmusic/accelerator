# Topology Behavioral Distinctions (MPF-TOPO-DIST-001)

## 1. Purpose
This document establishes the categorical distinctions between core topology behaviors within the Mono-Process Framework. To maintain mathematical and governance precision, it is mandatory to prevent the "semantic collapse" of these distinct categories. Behavioral categories may interact operationally without becoming interchangeable primitives.

## 2. Mandatory Status & Scope
All behavioral classifications and distinction rules MUST be explicitly marked with:
- **NOT_PROVEN**: Status is unverified.
- **STRICTLY_LOCAL**: Applies only to finite domains.
- **NON_PHYSICAL_ANALOG_MODEL**: No physical reality or universal law is implied.

## 3. Mandatory Distinctions

### 3.1 transition vs. reconfiguration
- **transition**: Changes topology state classification (e.g., moves from expansion to contraction).
- **reconfiguration**: Rearranges internal relations within a fixed state classification.
- **Key Difference**: Transition changes the *class*; reconfiguration rearranges the *content*.

### 3.2 corridor vs. path
- **corridor**: A persistent, constrained admissibility structure. It defines traversable landscapes.
- **path**: A specific traversal instance. It is the sequence of states or the active trajectory through a corridor.
- **Key Difference**: Corridor is the *structure*; path is the *event*.

### 3.3 orientation vs. transport
- **orientation**: A local relational field that constrains admissibility directionality.
- **transport**: Describes active movement behavior or propagation through defined paths.
- **Key Difference**: Orientation is the *constraint*; transport is the *dynamics*.

### 3.4 failure vs. invalidity
- **failure**: Bounded structural degradation where topology relationships break down locally.
- **invalidity**: A violation of governance or admissibility rules (e.g., a globalization attempt).
- **Key Difference**: Failure is *structural*; invalidity is *non-compliant*.

### 3.5 boundary vs. termination
- **boundary**: A governed admissibility condition that constrains continuation.
- **termination**: The cessation of admissible continuation (e.g., due to budget exhaustion).
- **Key Difference**: Boundary is the *rule*; termination is the *stop*.

### 3.6 admissibility vs. continuation
- **admissibility**: Defines allowed possibility conditions within a region.
- **continuation**: Describes the active persistence behavior of the process.
- **Key Difference**: Admissibility is the *allowance*; continuation is the *action*.

## 4. Semantic Anti-Collapse Rules
The framework enforces these rules to prevent categorical erosion:
- Behavioral categories must not be recursively defined only in terms of each other.
- No behavioral class may become a "universal placeholder" primitive.
- Orientation must not be silently treated as transport.
- Corridor must not be silently treated as an unrestricted path.
- Failure must not be silently treated as invalidity.
- Admissibility must not be silently treated as guaranteed continuation.

## 5. Language Restrictions
The following terms are **STRICTLY FORBIDDEN**:
- "all topology behaviors are equivalent"
- "universal topology primitive"
- "single master topology behavior"
- "globalized behavioral closure"
- "proof of behavioral unification"
