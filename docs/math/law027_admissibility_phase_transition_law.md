# Law-027: Admissibility Phase Transition Law

## 1. Definition
The **Admissibility Phase Transition Law** formalizes the sudden topology reorganization, tipping thresholds, avalanches, and regime shifts that occur in the Mono-Process Framework when admissibility-conditioned thresholds are crossed. It defines why metastable continuation organizations may abruptly dissolve or reorganize without assuming physical phase transitions or thermodynamic criticality.

## 2. Formal Statement
Within the recursive continuation framework:

- **Orientation Array**: {-(i)_α}
- **Metastable Regime**: $M_U$ (As defined in LAW-026)
- **Transition Pressure Candidate**: $P_T(M_U) := F(boundary\_pressure, budget\_depletion, perturbation\_load, reinforcement\_decay, basin\_competition)$ is the operational measure of instability load acting on a regime.
- **Tipping Threshold Candidate**: $\Theta_T(M_U)$ is the limit beyond which the current metastable regime structure can no longer satisfy admissibility requirements.
- **Phase Transition Condition**: $PhaseShift(M_U)$ occurs when $P_T(M_U) \ge \Theta_T(M_U)$ under declared transition tolerance.
- **Avalanche Condition**: $Avalanche(M_U)$ may occur when a transition in one basin or channel propagates instability through shared accessibility, budget, or reinforcement dependencies, triggering a cascading reorganization.
- **Topology Reorganization Condition**: $Reorganize(Top_A(U))$ occurs when prior accessibility relations $\sim_A$ no longer preserve admissibility-compatible continuation, forcing the stabilization of a new topology.
- **Regime Shift Condition**: $RegimeShift(M_U \to M_V)$ occurs when one metastable law-like regime loses validity and another stabilizers within the validity window.

## 3. Core Principles
- **Threshold-Driven Reorganization**: Changes in the metastable state are often abrupt and triggered by crossing specific operational thresholds.
- **Non-Linear Stability**: Stability is not a continuous gradient; regimes remain coherent until a tipping point is reached.
- **Instability Cascades**: Because continuation structures are interconnected through shared budgets and channels, local failure can propagate into regional "avalanches."
- **Topology Precedence**: The transition is fundamentally a reorganization of admissibility topology ($\sim_A$) rather than a change in a primitive background geometric space.

## 4. Governance & Limits
- **No Physics Claim**: This law defines phase transitions within the Mono-Process Framework and does not claim to describe physical phase changes (e.g., solid to liquid) or critical phenomena. There is no physical phase transition claim made here.
- **No Thermodynamic Criticality**: Concepts like entropy, temperature, or energy-minimization-as-physical-truth are explicitly blocked.
- **No Universal Regime Law**: The framework does not assume that all transitions follow the same universal scaling laws or universality classes.
- **No Primitive Geometry**: Transitions are described as topological reorganization events, not as changes in a background spatial coordinate system.
- **Threshold Dependency**: Transitions must be traceable to measurable operational pressures and finite resource constraints.

## 5. Failure Modes
- **Physical Phase-Transition Overclaim**: Mapping $PhaseShift(M_U)$ directly to physical matter states.
- **Thermodynamic Criticality Leakage**: Importing statistical mechanics or thermodynamics as the governing proof.
- **Universal Regime Law Overclaim**: Asserting that all regimes must transition according to a single global rule.
- **Transition Without Threshold**: Modeling shifts as continuous or random without identifiable operational tipping points.
- **Avalanche Without Dependency**: Modeling cascades without specifying the shared budget or accessibility links that propagate failure.
- **Topology Reorganization Without Admissibility**: Describing reorganization as a primitive geometric change rather than an admissibility-conditioned event.
- **Primitive Geometry Reintroduction**: Using "coordinates" or "distances" as the primary drivers of transition.
- **Physics Claim Leakage**: Citing these dynamics as a validation of physical phase transition theory.
