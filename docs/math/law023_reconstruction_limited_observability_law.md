# Law-023: Reconstruction-Limited Observability Law

## 1. Definition
The **Reconstruction-Limited Observability Law** defines local observability as an emergent, reconstruction-limited property of continuation topology. It formalizes why observation is always partial, bounded, and conditioned by accessibility horizons, reconstruction asymmetry, and continuation compression.

## 2. Formal Statement
Within the recursive continuation framework:

- **Orientation Array**: {-(i)_α}
- **Local Reconstruction Operator**: $\Xi_\alpha$ (As defined in LAW-016)
- **Observable Subset Candidate**: $Obs_\alpha(U)$ is the set of loci $\beta \in U$ that are reconstructively accessible from $\alpha$:
  $$Obs_\alpha(U) := \{ \beta \in U : \beta \in D_A(\alpha), F_\Xi(\alpha, \beta) \ge \theta_\Xi, A_\Xi(\alpha, \beta) \le \eta_\Xi \}$$
- **Reconstruction Fidelity**: $F_\Xi(\alpha, \beta)$ measures the strength of the reconstruction link, conditioned by accessibility, admissibility margin, compression loss, and perturbation corruption.
- **Ambiguity Region**: $Amb_\alpha$ is the set of loci where multiple distinct continuation structures project to equivalent reconstructed states, creating "observational indistinguishability."

### Hidden Topology Clause
Stable continuation topology and process structures may exist outside the observable subset $Obs_\alpha$ without being reconstructively accessible to $\alpha$.

### Bounded Observability Clause
Observability is fundamentally local, bounded, reconstruction-limited, and accessibility-conditioned. There is no "raw" or "direct" access to the full continuation array.

### Non-Observer Absolutism Clause
The framework assumes no universal observer, no complete reconstruction frame, and no global observation standpoint. All "observations" are themselves process-internal reconstruction events.

## 3. Core Principles
- **Observability as Process**: Observation is not a background primitive; it is a specific class of reconstruction process ($\Xi_\alpha$).
- **Bounded Fidelity**: The fidelity of observation decays with distance (accessibility), recursion depth (loss), and turbulence (perturbation).
- **Indistinguishability Regions**: Regions of high compression or loss create ambiguity where distinct process states appear equivalent.
- **Epistemic Horizons**: The reachable domain $D_A(\alpha)$ defines the ultimate limit of observability for any local process.

## 4. Governance & Limits
- **No Physics Claim**: This law defines observability within the Mono-Process Framework and does not claim to describe physical observation, measurement, or optics.
- **No Consciousness Theory**: Observability is an operational reconstruction property and does not imply awareness, sentience, or consciousness.
- **No Global Observer**: The reintroduction of a "view from nowhere" or universal coordinate frame is explicitly blocked.
- **No Complete Reconstruction**: The framework assumes that all reconstruction is subject to some level of loss ($\Xi \ne I$).
- **No Information Substance**: Observability is a relation between process structures, not the transfer of a primitive "information" fluid.

## 5. Failure Modes
- **Global Observer Overclaim**: Assuming a single observation can access the entire orientation array.
- **Complete Reconstruction Overclaim**: Neglecting the inherent loss and ambiguity in reconstruction processes.
- **Observer Absolutism Leakage**: Treating an observation as an absolute, structure-independent truth.
- **Hidden Topology Denial**: Assuming that if a structure is not observed by $\alpha$, it does not exist.
- **Lossless Observability Assumption**: Treating $\Xi$ as an identity operator for accessible regions.
- **Primitive Information Substance Reintroduction**: Modeling observability as the movement of irreducible information bits.
- **Consciousness Claim Leakage**: Mapping $Obs_\alpha$ to mental states or phenomenal experience.
