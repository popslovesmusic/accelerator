# Law-033: Hidden Topology and Inaccessible Continuation Domains Law

## 1. Definition
The **Hidden Topology and Inaccessible Continuation Domains Law** formalizes the existence of stable continuation structures that reside outside the local reconstruction or accessibility reach of a given process locus. It defines "hiddenness" not as a metaphysical property or a hidden variable, but as a bounded consequence of finite admissibility, fragmentation, compression, and reconstruction asymmetry.

## 2. Formal Statement
Within the recursive continuation framework:

- **Orientation Array**: {-(i)_α}
- **Accessible Domain ($D_A(\alpha)$)**: The set of loci reachable from $\alpha$ under finite transport flux and defined admissibility ($LAW-018$).
- **Hidden Domain Candidate ($H_A(\alpha)$)**: $H_A(\alpha) := \{ \beta : \beta \notin D_A(\alpha) \text{ or } reconstruction\_fidelity(\alpha, \beta) < \theta_\Xi \}$. This identifies loci that are topologicaly unreachable or reconstructively opaque.
- **Hidden Topology Candidate ($Top_H(U)$)**: The continuation topology that is weakly accessible, inaccessible, compressed, fragmented, or reconstructively ambiguous relative to a region $U$.
- **Inaccessibility Condition**: Inaccessibility arises through finite-flux decay, admissibility horizon formation ($LAW-018$), topology fragmentation ($LAW-032$), perturbation corruption, or arbitration pruning ($LAW-029$).
- **Partial Trace Condition**: A hidden domain may leave indirect traces, perturbation signatures, compression residues, or invariant distortions within an accessible domain without being directly reconstructible.
- **Non-Absolute Hiddenness Clause**: Hiddenness is fundamentally local and reconstruction-conditioned. What is hidden from locus $\alpha$ may be accessible from locus $\beta$ if $\alpha \notin D_A(\beta)$. Hiddenness is not an absolute or metaphysically privileged state.
- **Non-Observer Completion Clause**: No observer, reconstruction process, or admissibility domain is assumed capable of complete global topology recovery. Global observability is explicitly blocked.

## 3. Core Principles
- **Bounded Accessibility**: Reachability is finite; the framework intrinsically generates "hidden" regions as a result of its own resource and topology constraints.
- **Reconstruction Limits**: Even if a locus is topologically connected, it remains "hidden" if the fidelity of reconstruction ($\Xi$) falls below the operational threshold.
- **Relational Reality**: Inaccessible domains are as "real" as accessible ones in terms of their contribution to topological invariants, but they are operationally distinct.
- **Traces and Residue**: Hidden structures influence accessible domains through indirect residues and boundary pressures rather than direct interaction.

## 4. Governance & Limits
- **No Physics Claim**: This law defines hidden domains within the Mono-Process Framework and does not claim to describe physical "hidden variables," dark matter, or parallel universes.
- **No Hidden Variable Claim**: This is not a "hidden variable" theory in the sense of quantum mechanics; hiddenness is a structural limit of reconstruction, not a deterministic substrate.
- **No Metaphysical External World**: The framework does not assume an irreducible "external world" independent of the process continuation array.
- **No Global Observability**: The assumption that a privileged viewpoint exists from which the entire topology is visible is explicitly blocked.
- **No Primitive External Reality**: Hidden domains are emergent properties of the orientation array, not a background substance.
- **Reconstruction Dependency**: Hiddenness must be shown to emerge from specific accessibility horizons and reconstruction fidelity limits.

## 5. Failure Modes
- **Hidden Variable Overclaim**: Using $Top_H$ to reintroduce deterministic hidden variables to explain local ambiguity.
- **Metaphysical External World Overclaim**: Asserting that hidden domains constitute a different "kind" of reality.
- **Complete Global Reconstruction Overclaim**: Claiming that global topology can be recovered by aggregating local observations.
- **Absolute Hiddenness Overclaim**: Modeling hiddenness as a permanent or universal property rather than a local relational one.
- **Perfect Observability Overclaim**: Assuming everything within a certain radius is perfectly reconstructible.
- **Inaccessibility Without Budget or Topology**: Modeling hiddenness as a random effect rather than a consequence of specific process limits.
- **Primitive External Reality Reintroduction**: Treating hidden domains as a pre-existing container for the process.
- **Physics Claim Leakage**: Citing hidden topology as a validation of physical dark-sector theories.
