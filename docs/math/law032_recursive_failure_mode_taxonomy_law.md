# Law-032: Recursive Failure Mode Taxonomy Law

## 1. Definition
The **Recursive Failure Mode Taxonomy Law** formalizes the classification and governing conditions of failure states within the Mono-Process Framework. It defines failure modes (runaway, deadlock, fragmentation, lock, collapse, cascade, and reconstruction failure) as first-class structural events, ensuring that process instability is preserved as a governed outcome rather than being suppressed by stabilization language.

## 2. Formal Statement
Within the recursive continuation framework:

- **Orientation Array**: {-(i)_α}
- **Failure Mode Family ($Fail_A$)**: The set of distinguishable failure states: $\{runaway, deadlock, fragmentation, reinforcement\_lock, admissibility\_collapse, budget\_exhaustion, perturbation\_cascade, reconstruction\_failure\}$.
- **Runaway Condition**: Occurs when recursive reinforcement ($LAW-015$) or perturbation amplification ($LAW-022$) exceeds the limits of bounded admissibility control ($LAW-021$), leading to unstable growth.
- **Deadlock Condition**: Occurs when competing continuation candidates in $Q_\alpha$ ($LAW-029$) cannot be arbitrated without violating budget, admissibility, or topological invariant constraints.
- **Fragmentation Condition**: Occurs when orientation-array accessibility topology ($\sim_A$) separates into mutually unreachable or weakly reconstructible components, breaking regional coherence.
- **Reinforcement Lock Condition**: Occurs when a continuation channel ($C_P$) becomes over-stabilized, suppressing all other admissible continuation pathways beyond declared tolerance.
- **Admissibility Collapse Condition**: Occurs when continuation demand exceeds the local admissibility budget ($B_A$) and no valid projection ($\Pi_A$), pruning, or redirection remains.
- **Cascade Condition**: Occurs when the failure of one basin, channel, or regime propagates instability through shared resource or accessibility dependencies, triggering regional collapse.
- **Reconstruction Failure Condition**: Occurs when recursive compression, loss, or ambiguity prevents the reconstruction ($\Xi$) of structural properties required for future continuation.

## 3. Core Principles
- **Failure as Structure**: Failure is not the "end" of the process but a specific, governed transition into an unstable or non-reconstructible state.
- **Dependency Tracking**: Failures are traceable to underlying resource limits (budget), topology (accessibility), and history (reinforcement).
- **Non-Suppression**: The framework must explicitly record and preserve failure modes rather than assuming perfect stabilization.
- **Operational Distinguishability**: Different failure modes have distinct operational signatures and propagation mechanics.

## 4. Governance & Limits
- **No Physics Claim**: This law defines failure modes within the Mono-Process Framework and does not claim to describe physical failure (e.g., material fracture, structural collapse) or biological death.
- **No False Stability**: The assumption that any continuation structure can achieve perfect, immutable stability is blocked. Modeling results that "hide" failure through forced stabilization are explicitly blocked.
- **No Perfect Stability**: There is no state of absolute, eternal stability in the framework; all structures are metastable and subject to failure mode conditions.
- **No Universal Catastrophe Theory**: MPF does not import global catastrophe or singularity theories as governing proofs.
- **Constraint Dependency**: All failure modes must be shown to emerge from the exhaustion or violation of specific admissibility, budget, or reconstruction constraints.

## 5. Failure Modes (of the Law itself)
- **Failure Mode Suppression**: Ignoring or renaming collapse events to avoid recording "failure."
- **False Stability Overclaim**: Asserting that a regime is stable when it is in a state of reinforcement lock or impending cascade.
- **Catastrophe Theory Leakage**: Using general catastrophe theory to bypass the specific operational mechanics of the framework.
- **Physics Failure Claim Leakage**: Citing MPF failure modes as a validation of physical disaster theories.
- **Complete Stability Classification Overclaim**: Claiming a final and complete classification of all possible process instabilities.
- **Collapse Without Budget Context**: Modeling collapse as a random event rather than a consequence of budget exhaustion.
- **Fragmentation Without Accessibility Context**: Describing fragmentation without identifying the break in orientation-array topology.
- **Deadlock Without Arbitration Context**: Modeling deadlock without identifying the competing candidates and conflicting constraints.
