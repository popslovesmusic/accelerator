# Identity as Process Continuity

## 1. Purpose
This document clarifies the ontological status of "identity" within the Mono-Process Framework. It explicitly defines identity as **operational continuity** and rejects the reification of persistent structures into permanent "objects."

## 2. The Doctrine of Operational Continuity
Identity is not an intrinsic property of a "thing." It is the **successful, recursive continuation of a process pattern through change**.

### 2.1 Identity vs. Permanence
- **Object Permanence (Forbidden):** The classical notion that an object exists as an independent substance that endures through time.
- **Operational Continuity (Correct):** The ability of a metastable process pattern (like a continuation channel) to successfully reproduce its own defining constraints through successive process steps.

### 2.2 Identity as a Verb
Identity in this framework is a **verb**: a structure "identities" by successfully continuing. If continuation ceases, or if the process transitions to a new regime, the former identity is lost.

## 3. Link to MT-LAW-A
The Bounded Continuation Persistence lemma (MT-LAW-A) is the formal model for this principle. "Persistence" is the measure of how long a structure can maintain its operational continuity before being forced by perturbations or budget exhaustion to transition.
