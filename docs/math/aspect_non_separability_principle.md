# Aspect Non-Separability Principle (MPF-PALG-005-REFORM)

## 1. Purpose
This document defines the **Aspect Non-Separability Principle** as a candidate foundational principle within the Mono-Process Framework. 
- **Source Relation**: $(E \neq 0) \iff_R \delta(E > 0)$
- **Non-Separability Acknowledged**: True. Aspects are analytically distinguishable but not fundamental.
 It replaces previous proposals for fragmented composition operators with a stricter rule of indivisibility: recursive continuation aspects are locally distinguishable but not independently fundamental outside their whole relation.

## 2. Principle Definition

### 2.1 Plain Language
Recursive continuation aspects (such as the exclusion-pole, continuation-pole, and the relation itself) are distinguishable for analysis but are not independently complete or fundamental. They are "aspects" of one process structure rather than separable primitives.

### 2.2 Symbolic Seed
The relation $\boxed{ A \iff_R B }$ is treated as an indivisible whole.

### 2.3 Formal Interpretation (Candidate)
- **Non-Separability**: $[A \iff_R B] \neq [A] + [\iff_R] + [B]$. The structure cannot be reduced to its parts.
- **Aspect Form**: Each component is formally an aspect of the same "WholeR" process.
- **Projection Rule**: Analytical separation is permitted ONLY as a restricted convenience for projection and mapping.
- **Ontological Rule**: No separated aspect may be treated as independently complete or fundamental.
- **Non-Fragmentation**: The introduction of separate secondary operators is treated as suspect decomposition unless absolute necessity is demonstrated.

## 3. Usage Rules

### 3.1 Allowed Uses
- Interpreting $(E \neq 0)$, $\delta(E > 0)$, and $\iff_R$ as simultaneous aspects of one closure relation.
- Interpreting $-1$, $+1$, and $\iff_R$ as aspects of recursive polarity rather than isolated numeric objects.
- Analyzing process aspects provisionally without granting them independent primitive status.

### 3.2 Forbidden Uses
- Treating $\iff_R$ as an "external glue" operator connecting independent objects.
- Treating process aspects as fully independent or separable primitives.
- Using non-separability as "proof" of physical nonlocality or QM/GR mathematical unification.

## 4. Example Interpretations
- **Closure**: Distinction-generation, continuation-pressure, and residue-binding are simultaneous aspects of one process closure.
- **Polarity**: Recursive polarity is indivisible; the poles are not independently complete.
- **Analogy**: Permitted only as a restricted projection analogy where QM-like and GR-like domains are aspects of a deeper continuation structure.
