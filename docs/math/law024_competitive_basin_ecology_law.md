# Law-024: Competitive Basin Ecology Law

## 1. Definition
The **Competitive Basin Ecology Law** defines the interaction dynamics between stabilized reconciliation basins under finite continuation resources. It formalizes how basins compete for admissibility, transport flux, and reinforcement pathways, leading to emergent behaviors such as starvation, cannibalization, co-stabilization, and collapse propagation.

## 2. Formal Statement
Within the recursive continuation framework:

- **Orientation Array**: {-(i)_α}
- **Basin Family**: $\mathfrak{B}_U := \{ B_i : B_i \text{ is an active stabilized reconciliation basin in region } U \}$
- **Basin Overlap Condition**: $Overlap(B_i, B_j)$ holds when basins share accessibility domains, reinforcement channels, transport flux, or admissibility budget.
- **Competition Condition**: $Compete(B_i, B_j)$ occurs when the simultaneous persistence of $B_i$ and $B_j$ exceeds the shared local admissibility budget $B_A(U)$ or destabilizes boundary compatibility.
- **Starvation Condition**: $Starve(B_i)$ occurs when reinforcement inflow, admissibility margin, or transport accessibility falls below the required threshold for basin persistence.
- **Cannibalization Condition**: $Cannibalize(B_i, B_j)$ occurs when the reinforcement and stabilization of $B_i$ redirects or consumes continuation support that previously stabilized $B_j$.
- **Co-Stabilization Condition**: $CoStabilize(B_i, B_j)$ occurs when basins share compatible accessibility topology and mutual reinforcement, reducing drift or boundary pressure for both.
- **Collapse Propagation Condition**: $Collapse(B_i)$ may propagate to $B_j$ if the failure of $B_i$ depletes a shared budget, disrupts stabilizing channels, or increases the perturbation load on neighboring structures.

### Non-Biological Clause
Basin ecology is a formal structural analogy for interacting continuation basins. It does not claim to describe biological ecology, natural selection, or evolutionary dynamics.

### No Global Selection Clause
The law defines local interaction dynamics and does not imply a global selection rule, a universal "survival of the fittest," or a teleological drive toward optimal basin configurations.

## 3. Core Principles
- **Resource Finite-ness**: Interaction is driven by the finiteness of the admissibility budget and transport capacity (LAW-021).
- **Topology-Conditioned Interaction**: Competition and co-stabilization are governed by accessibility horizons and orientation-array topology.
- **Dynamic Equilibrium**: "Ecology" refers to a state of dynamic, multi-basin stability or instability, not a static terminal state.
- **Cascading Failure**: The interconnectedness of basins through shared channels (LAW-014) makes collapse propagation a fundamental structural risk.

## 4. Governance & Limits
- **No Physics Claim**: This law describes basin dynamics within the Mono-Process Framework and does not claim to describe physical systems, fluid dynamics, or thermodynamic phases.
- **No Biological Ecology**: Mapping these dynamics to biological organisms, ecosystems, or genetic selection is explicitly blocked.
- **No Global Optimality**: The framework does not assume that competitive outcomes result in "better" or more "optimal" states.
- **No Force Single Winner**: The dynamics do not require a single dominant basin; stable multi-basin ecologies are permitted.
- **Finite Budget Dependency**: All interaction effects must be traceable to finite admissibility, transport, or reinforcement constraints.

## 5. Failure Modes
- **Biological Ecology Overclaim**: Treating basins as if they were biological entities with goals, survival instincts, or genetic information.
- **Global Selection Overclaim**: Assuming a global rule determines which basins persist.
- **Single Winner Collapse**: Artificially forcing a "winner-takes-all" dynamic when co-stabilization is admissible.
- **Unbounded Basin Support Assumption**: Assuming basins can persist regardless of local budget depletion.
- **Competition Without Budget Constraint**: Modeling interaction as a primitive "force" rather than a consequence of shared finite resources.
- **Collapse Propagation Suppression**: Ignoring the risk that the failure of one basin can destabilize its neighbors.
- **Primitive Law Reintroduction**: Treating basin "ecology" as an irreducible governing law rather than an emergent interaction dynamic.
- **Physics Claim Leakage**: Citing these dynamics as a validation of physical ecology or environmental science.
