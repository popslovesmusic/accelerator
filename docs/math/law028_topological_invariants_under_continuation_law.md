# Law-028: Topological Invariants Under Continuation Law

## 1. Definition
The **Topological Invariants Under Continuation Law** defines candidate invariants that are preserved across recursive continuation transformations, topology mutation, basin transition, channel reorganization, and admissibility phase shifts. It identifies what relational properties remain stable even as the underlying process organizations reorganize, providing a basis for emergent structural continuity.

## 2. Formal Statement
Within the recursive continuation framework:

- **Orientation Array**: {-(i)_α}
- **Invariant Candidate Family**: $Inv_A(U) := \{ I_k : I_k \text{ remains stable under admissibility-compatible continuation transformations over region } U \}$.
- **Persistence Invariant Candidate ($I_p$)**: Operational continuity of a stabilized basin or channel maintained above threshold under bounded drift.
- **Accessibility Invariant Candidate ($I_a$)**: Preserved reachability-equivalence class among orientation-array loci even under local topology mutation.
- **Reinforcement Invariant Candidate ($I_r$)**: Retained reinforcement-overlap $H(C_P, n) \cap H(C_P, n+k)$ above declared tolerance across recursion depth.
- **Reconstruction Invariant Candidate ($I_\Xi$)**: Preserved reconstruction-equivalence relation $\sim_{obs}$ under bounded recursive compression and structural loss.
- **Invariant Failure Condition**: Invariant failure occurs when topology transition, perturbation, decay, or budget saturation breaks the declared preservation tolerance for a specific candidate $I_k$.
- **Non-Global Invariant Clause**: Invariants are fundamentally local, conditional, and admissibility-bounded. No universal invariant or global conservation law is claimed.

## 3. Core Principles
- **Relational Stability**: Invariants are properties of relations and organizations, not of primitive substances or objects.
- **Bounded Invariance**: Preservation is valid only within declared admissibility windows and reconstruction tolerances.
- **Emergent Conservation**: What appears as "conservation" is the result of stabilized continuation structure resisting reorganization.
- **Tolerance-Conditioned Continuity**: Continuity is not absolute but is defined by the stability of invariants under a specific set of transformations.

## 4. Governance & Limits
- **No Physics Claim**: This law defines topological invariants within the Mono-Process Framework and does not claim to describe physical conservation laws (e.g., energy, momentum, charge).
- **No Global Conservation**: There is no claim of a globally preserved quantity or a universal closure condition for the entire orientation array.
- **No Universal Invariance**: No property is assumed to be invariant under all possible continuation transformations.
- **No Complete Topology Theorem**: This law identifies candidates for invariance and does not provide a formal proof of universal topological closure.
- **No Reconstruction Loss Suppression**: Invariants must be defined in a way that preserves the reality of structural loss ($Loss(C_P, n)$) and reconstruction asymmetry.
- **Conditional Tolerance Dependency**: All claims of invariance must specify the admissibility and reconstruction tolerances required for their preservation.

## 5. Failure Modes
- **Global Conservation Overclaim**: Asserting that a quantity is conserved across the entire framework.
- **Universal Invariant Overclaim**: Assuming a relational property is invariant regardless of the transformation.
- **Physics Invariant Leakage**: Mapping topological invariants directly to physical conservation laws.
- **Invariance Without Tolerance**: Modeling preservation as absolute rather than bounded and conditional.
- **Topology Change Denial**: Using "invariance" to ignore or suppress the reality of sudden regime shifts or topology reorganization.
- **Perfect Identity Preservation Overclaim**: Assuming identity ($Id_A$) is perfectly preserved across all mutations.
- **Reconstruction Loss Suppression**: Defining invariants that ignore the accumulation of structural loss.
- **Global Closure Overclaim**: Claiming that the orientation array forms a closed topological system with fixed global invariants.
