# The Observer as a Local Process Projection

## 1. Purpose
This document clarifies the ontological status of the "observer" in the Mono-Process Framework. It explicitly rejects the concept of a privileged, external viewpoint and instead frames observation as a local, budget-constrained process projection.

## 2. The Doctrine of Local Observation
An "observation" or "measurement" is not a passive reading of a pre-existing state. It is an **active, admissible interaction** that is itself a process governed by the same core rules.

### 2.1 Observation as a Projection
- **No External Viewpoint:** There is no "view from nowhere." Any act of observation is a process occurring within the framework.
- **Projection, Not Revelation:** An observation is a **local process projection** ($Proj_{obs}$). It constructs a bounded, context-dependent representation of a process aspect; it does not "reveal" an absolute, independent reality.

### 2.2 The Observer Process
An "observer" is a metastable structure whose continuation depends on successfully creating and processing these observational projections. Like any other structure, it is subject to:
- **Finite Admissibility Budget ($B_A$):** Observation has a cost and is resource-limited.
- **Limited Reachability:** An observer can only interact with its local, admissible domain.
- **Reconstruction Loss:** As formalized in Patch 036, information about states beyond the observer's accessibility horizon or before a major phase transition is permanently lost.

## 3. Rejection of Privileged Frames
This doctrine strictly forbids any claims based on privileged, absolute, or cost-free observation. All knowledge within the framework is local, contingent, and derived from finite process interaction.
