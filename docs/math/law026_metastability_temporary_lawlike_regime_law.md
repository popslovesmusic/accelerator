# Law-026: Metastability and Temporary Law-Like Regime Law

## 1. Definition
The **Metastability and Temporary Law-Like Regime Law** formalizes the existence of law-like regimes as metastable, locally valid continuation-channel organizations. It defines why "laws" (as emergent persistent channels) possess finite validity windows and how they undergo transitions between distinct metastable states without assuming eternal or universal governing rules.

## 2. Formal Statement
Within the recursive continuation framework:

- **Orientation Array**: {-(i)_α}
- **Metastable Regime Candidate**: $M_U := \{ C_i \in U : C_i \text{ remains reinforced, admissible, and mutually coherent within tolerance over bounded continuation depth} \}$
- **Validity Window Candidate**: $V(M_U)$ is the bounded interval or order-depth over which the metastable regime $M_U$ satisfies required persistence, budget, and topology-coherence conditions.
- **Lifespan Condition**: $Life(M_U)$ is the duration (in continuation depth) for which the regime remains stable, determined by reinforcement renewal, decay rate ($\lambda$), perturbation load, and admissibility budget exhaustion.
- **Transition Condition**: $Transition(M_U \to M_V)$ occurs when structural decay, cumulative perturbation, or topology transition (fracture/merge) causes $M_U$ to lose coherence or admissibility while a new coherent organization $M_V$ stabilizes.
- **Temporary Law Clause**: Law-like regimes are fundamentally locally valid and metastable. They are structural properties of a specific continuation context and are not eternal governing rules or universal constants.

## 3. Core Principles
- **Metastability as Norm**: Persistence is the result of active stabilization; all law-like organizations are metastable and subject to transition.
- **Validity Windows**: Every law-like regime has a finite boundary in the orientation array and in continuation depth.
- **Regime Shifts**: Transitions between regimes are structural reorganization events driven by the failure of prior stabilization structures.
- **Local Necessity**: Within a validity window, a regime may appear necessary and "law-like," but this necessity is contingent on the underlying reinforcement history.

## 4. Governance & Limits
- **No Physics Claim**: This law defines metastability within the Mono-Process Framework and does not claim to describe physical phase transitions, cosmic eras, or the "laws of physics."
- **No Eternal Laws**: The assumption that some law-like structures are immune to decay or valid across all possible continuation contexts is explicitly blocked.
- **No Universal Regime Structure**: The framework does not claim that a single regime-type is universal or that all regions must stabilize into the same regime.
- **No Global Equilibrium**: Stability is local and metastable; there is no assumption of a global "equilibrium" state for the entire orientation array.
- **Decay Dependency**: Regime transitions must be traceable to underlying decay (LAW-025) and budget (LAW-021) constraints.

## 5. Failure Modes
- **Eternal Law Overclaim**: Asserting that a law-like channel is eternal and exists independently of active reinforcement.
- **Universal Regime Overclaim**: Assuming a single metastable regime governs the entire orientation array.
- **Permanent Metastability Overclaim**: Modeling metastability as if it were absolute stability that never fails.
- **Regime Transition Without Decay**: Modeling transitions as random jumps without accounting for structural erosion or resource depletion.
- **Law-Like Validity Without Boundary**: Ignoring the validity window limits of a regime.
- **Primitive Law Reintroduction**: Treating a regime as an irreducible law rather than an emergent organization of channels.
- **Global Equilibrium Leakage**: Using thermodynamic equilibrium concepts to explain regime stability.
- **Physics Claim Leakage**: Citing metastable regimes as a validation of physical laws or cosmology.
