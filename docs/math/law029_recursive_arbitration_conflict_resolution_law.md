# Law-029: Recursive Arbitration and Conflict Resolution Law

## 1. Definition
The **Recursive Arbitration and Conflict Resolution Law** formalizes how the Mono-Process Framework resolves conflicts among competing continuation possibilities. When multiple admissible structures (branches, channels, basins, or regimes) compete for finite resources, local arbitration determines which subset persists, splits, merges, or collapses without requiring deterministic global selection or universal optimality.

## 2. Formal Statement
Within the recursive continuation framework:

- **Orientation Array**: {-(i)_α}
- **Candidate Set**: $Q_\alpha := \{ q_i : q_i \text{ is an admissible continuation candidate at or reachable from } \alpha \}$.
- **Arbitration Operator Candidate**: $Arb_A(Q_\alpha)$ is the operator that determines the selected, deferred, split, merged, pruned, or collapsed continuation subset under local admissibility constraints.
- **Conflict Condition**: $Conflict(Q_\alpha)$ occurs when the simultaneous persistence of candidates in $Q_\alpha$ exceeds the local admissibility budget $B_A$, violates boundary compatibility, or destabilizes preserved topological invariants ($Inv_A$).
- **Priority Score Candidate**: $Priority_A(q_i) := F(admissibility\_margin, reinforcement\_support, finite\_flux\_cost, perturbation\_resilience, invariant\_preservation, reconstruction\_accessibility)$ is the operational measure used to rank candidates.
- **Tie-Resolution Condition**: $Tie(Q_\alpha)$ may preserve multi-branch continuation, defer selection, invoke local tie-break rules, or maintain structural ambiguity when priority scores remain equivalent within tolerance.
- **Non-Unique Arbitration Clause**: Arbitration is not assumed to be unique, deterministic, globally optimal, or final.
- **Recursive Feedback Clause**: The outcomes of arbitration feed back into future local budgets, reinforcement history, and topology transition dynamics.

## 3. Core Principles
- **Resource-Driven Selection**: Arbitration is a consequence of finite admissibility budgets (LAW-021) and finite transport flux (LAW-004).
- **Ambiguity Preservation**: In the absence of a clear priority differential, the framework allows for multi-valued or deferred continuation rather than forcing a random choice.
- **Feedback Loops**: Arbitration choices reorganize the local topology, influencing the admissibility of future continuation candidates.
- **Constraint Satisfaction**: Arbitration prioritizes the preservation of topological invariants (LAW-028) and the maintenance of stabilization basins (LAW-011).

## 4. Governance & Limits
- **No Physics Claim**: This law defines arbitration within the Mono-Process Framework and does not claim to describe physical causality, decision theory, or agent-based choice.
- **No Deterministic Selection**: The framework explicitly blocks claims of universal determinism; arbitration may result in irreducible ambiguity.
- **No Global Optimality**: Arbitration does not imply a "best" or "most optimal" selection according to a global rule.
- **No Force Single Winner**: The resolution process does not require a single "winner" if multi-branch continuation is admissible.
- **Finite Budget Dependency**: All arbitration events must be traceable to finite local admissibility and flux constraints.

## 5. Failure Modes
- **Deterministic Selection Overclaim**: Asserting that arbitration always yields a single, predictable outcome.
- **Global Optimality Overclaim**: Assuming arbitration results in a state of global maximum efficiency or "fitness."
- **Single Winner Collapse**: Artificially forcing the dissolution of all but one competing branch when multiple are admissible.
- **Arbitration Without Budget Constraint**: Modeling selection as a primitive law rather than a consequence of shared resource limits.
- **Tie-Resolution Overclaim**: Claiming a universal rule for breaking priority ties.
- **Invariant Violation Suppression**: Allowing arbitration outcomes that break declared topological invariants.
- **Primitive Law Reintroduction**: Treating arbitration as a top-down governing rule rather than an emergent interaction of constraints.
- **Physics Claim Leakage**: Citing arbitration dynamics as a validation of physical cause-and-effect or quantum collapse.
