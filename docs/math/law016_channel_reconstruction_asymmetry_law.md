# Law-016: Channel Reconstruction Asymmetry Law

## 1. Definition
The **Channel Reconstruction Asymmetry Law** formalizes why present continuation-channel structure cannot generally be used to uniquely reconstruct its full prior reinforcement history.

## 2. Formal Statement
Within the recursive continuation framework:

- **Orientation Array**: {-(i)_α}
- **Continuation Channel**: $C_P$
- **History Projection**: $H(C_P, n)$
- **Reconstruction Candidate**: Reconstruction Candidate $\Xi(C_P) := \text{admissibility-constrained reconstruction attempt from present continuation structure}$

### Asymmetry Condition
Asymmetry Condition: $\Xi(C_P)$ may yield multiple admissible prehistories or incomplete reconstruction under recursive continuation dynamics.

### Loss Accumulation Condition
Loss Accumulation Condition: $Loss(C_P, n)$ accumulates when continuation events compress, merge, redirect, fracture, or suppress prior reinforcement structure.

### Non-Unique Prehistory Clause
Non-Unique Prehistory Clause: Distinct reinforcement histories $\{H_1, H_2, \dots, H_k\}$ may project into observationally equivalent continuation-channel states $C_P$.

### Irreversibility Projection Clause
Irreversibility Projection Clause: Apparent irreversibility is treated as reconstruction asymmetry under bounded continuation accessibility.

## 3. Core Principles
- **Asymmetric Retention**: While channels retain traces of their reinforcement history (Law-015), the mapping from history to present structure is generally many-to-one (non-injective).
- **Information Loss**: Recursive continuation is a lossy process. Each reconciliation event and selection step can overwrite or discard prior structural details.
- **No Absolute Reversibility**: The framework does not assume a globally reversible primitive substrate.
- **Bounded Reconstruction**: Reconstruction is an active process ($\Xi$) subject to the same admissibility constraints as continuation.

## 4. Governance & Limits
- **No Physics Claim**: This law does not claim to derive physical laws of thermodynamics or entropy.
- **No Entropy Equivalence**: It does not establish equivalence between reconstruction loss and physical entropy.
- **No Unique Prehistory**: It explicitly denies that a unique prior state can always be recovered from the present.
- **Preserve Asymmetry**: The goal is to preserve the observation of asymmetry without introducing metaphysical necessity.

## 5. Failure Modes
- **Perfect History Reconstruction Overclaim**: Claiming that $H(C_P, n)$ can be fully recovered from $C_P$.
- **Unique Prehistory Overclaim**: Claiming that only one prior history could lead to the current $C_P$.
- **Entropy Equivalence Leakage**: Using thermodynamic language to justify reconstruction asymmetry.
- **Primitive Memory Reintroduction**: Treating $H(C_P, n)$ as a stored "object" rather than a projected trace.
