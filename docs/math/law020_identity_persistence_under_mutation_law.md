# Law-020: Identity Persistence Under Mutation Law

## 1. Definition
The **Identity Persistence Under Mutation Law** formalizes identity as an emergent operational continuity relation. It replaces the concept of primitive "object sameness" with a measure of persistence across continuation-channel mutation, bifurcation, and merging.

## 2. Formal Statement
Within the recursive continuation framework:

- **Orientation Array**: {-(i)_α}
- **Continuation Channel**: $C_P$
- **Mutated Channel**: $C_P'$
- **Identity Relation Candidate**: $Id_A(C_P, C_P')$ holds if and only if:
  1. $Continuity_A(C_P, C_P')$ (Operational continuity metric)
  2. $Drift(C_P, C_P') < \eta_{Id}$ (Mutation drift within tolerance)
  3. $ReinforcementOverlap(H(C_P), H(C_P')) \ge \theta_{Id}$ (Significant history overlap)
- **Continuity Metric**: $Continuity_A$ is a function of accessibility overlap, admissibility margin overlap, reinforcement history overlap, and reconstruction equivalence.

### Fork Condition
$Fork(C_P \rightarrow \{C_i\})$ occurs when multiple descendant channels preserve partial identity continuity above the declared threshold $\theta_{Id}$, leading to identity branching.

### Merge Condition
$Merge(\{C_i\} \rightarrow C_M)$ preserves composite identity only under declared ancestry and reconstruction equivalence conditions.

### Identity Collapse Condition
Identity collapse occurs when mutation drift, reconstruction loss ($\Xi$), or reinforcement history discontinuity exceeds the declared identity tolerance limits.

### Non-Primitive Identity Clause
Identity is an operational persistence relation (a "traceable continuation pathway") rather than a primitive object substance or static essence.

## 3. Core Principles
- **Continuity Over Sameness**: Identity is defined by the smoothness and reachability of the mutation path rather than an invariant "core."
- **History-Conditioned Persistence**: An identity relation requires recoverable overlap in reinforcement history ($H(C_P)$).
- **Tolerance Bounds**: Identity is relative to a declared tolerance $\eta_{Id}$ and $\theta_{Id}$, which may vary depending on the reconstruction scale.
- **Topology-Dependent Identity**: Identity is sensitive to the topology transitions of the orientation array (fracture, bifurcation, merge).

## 4. Governance & Limits
- **No Physics Claim**: This law defines operational identity within the Mono-Process Framework and does not claim to describe the persistence of physical objects.
- **No Primitive Identity**: Identity is not a primitive and cannot be used as an irreducible axiom.
- **No Personal Identity**: This law does not address metaphysical, biological, or psychological personal identity.
- **No Perfect Preservation**: Identity is subject to recursive loss and drift; "perfect" identity preservation is not assumed or claimed.
- **No Static Objects**: The framework remains a process-only system; objects are treated as stabilized continuation channels.

## 5. Failure Modes
- **Primitive Object Identity Reintroduction**: Treating $Id_A$ as an irreducible property of a "thing."
- **Substance Identity Overclaim**: Assuming identity depends on a persisting substance or essence.
- **Perfect Identity Continuity Overclaim**: Neglecting the role of drift and reconstruction loss.
- **Identity Without History Continuity**: Claiming identity between discontinuous reinforcement paths.
- **Merge Identity Overcollapse**: Erasing ancestry information in merge events prematurely.
- **Fork Identity Singleton Collapse**: Arbitrarily selecting one branch as the "true" successor.
- **Personal Identity Claim Leakage**: Applying process identity to sentient or biological agents.
