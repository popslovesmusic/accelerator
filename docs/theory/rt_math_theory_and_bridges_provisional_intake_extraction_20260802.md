## Part I — Mathematical theory extracted from the textbook

### 1. Process and the canonical whole expression

The program treats process as primary. The canonical root expression is:

\[
(\mathcal E\ne0)\ \Leftrightarrow_R\ \delta_a(\mathcal E>0).
\]

The expression is read as a residue-conditioned recursive relation: a non-zero distinction is admitted into a positive realization through the admissibility operator, with the recursive relation carrying the continuation condition. The left and right sides are not independent substances or stages. The whole expression has primacy over isolated symbols.

The textbook’s foundational chain is:

\[
\mathcal E\ne0\ \longrightarrow\ \mathcal E>0
\quad\text{under}\quad \delta_a
\quad\text{and}\quad \Leftrightarrow_R.
\]

The theory distinguishes the process-expression field from any projection made from it. A projection may expose a feature while losing other relational information; it therefore cannot be treated as the source expression itself.

### 2. Residue, memory, and recursive closure

Residue \(R\) records what remains structurally relevant after a distinction or update. Residue is not ordinary time and is not automatically a physical memory store. It conditions which continuations remain admissible.

The textbook uses residue inscription \(\Psi\) to represent the retention of relational consequences and recursive closure \(\Leftrightarrow_R\) to express continuation under that retained condition. A history-conditioned admissibility rule can therefore be written schematically as:

\[
\delta_a(x\mid R_{\le t})
\]

without identifying the residue with a temporal coordinate. The distinction between residue and time is maintained: time may be a projection or ordering parameter, while residue is the condition carried by the process.

The core consequence is bounded continuation: a later realization remains related to its prior conditions, but recoverability, identity, and reversibility are separate questions and are not granted by the notation alone.

### 3. Distinction and directed difference

The relational primitive is represented as:

\[
D(S_1\mid S_2).
\]

The vertical bar records a typed relational separation, not a scalar subtraction. Ordering carries structural information, so in general:

\[
D(S_1\mid S_2)\ne D(S_2\mid S_1).
\]

The distinction has value and operation aspects. A value is what is distinguished under the declared relation; an operation is the bounded procedure that evaluates or transforms that distinction. The floor condition prevents the relational result from being silently collapsed into zero identity. Below-floor outcomes are treated as projection or failure conditions, not as evidence that the underlying relation never existed.

The textbook also preserves a candidate non-identity relation \(<\ne>_X\). It is a formalization target, not a completed universal identity theorem.

### 4. Symmetry, asymmetry, and domains

Symmetry and asymmetry are treated as relational domains rather than as independent substances. A pre-scalar domain can contain a distinction before a numerical magnitude is assigned. Observation and orientation can induce a dual description without turning the description into a scalar measurement.

The asymmetry domain records ordered relational difference. The symmetry domain describes a limiting or collapse condition in which distinguishable relations may approach an untyped zero boundary. The textbook retains an explicit warning that symmetric collapse is not automatically a realized object or a physical singularity.

Triadic closure is used where two-sided distinction alone is insufficient for persistence. The recurring structural requirement is a third functional relation that prevents simultaneous collapse of the participating distinctions. This is the textbook-level mathematical expression of the 3-Peak stability rule, scoped to the framework’s relational model.

### 5. Orientation and direction

Orientation is the condition that selects a direction of relational evaluation. It is not time. The orientation operator \(-(i)\) and orientation-indexed update chains provide a way to distinguish a path through relational possibilities without treating the path as motion of a substance.

The textbook’s directionality principle is:

\[
\text{direction} = \text{oriented admissible continuation},
\]

not an automatically metric or physical vector. Reorientation changes the admissible continuation context. Recoupling, aligned asymmetry, relational clusters, and coupling neighborhoods are progressively richer structures for carrying that context.

### 6. Admissibility operators

The admissibility filter \(\delta_a\) selects continuations that satisfy the declared conditions. Directional variants \(\to_a\) and \(\gets_a\) allow the source and support direction to remain distinct.

An orientation-conditioned admissibility relation has the schematic form:

\[
\delta_a(x;O,C,R),
\]

where \(O\) is orientation, \(C\) is context, and \(R\) is residue. If the conditions are not met, the result is an empty-set or undefined-composition outcome. Failure is a typed result of the calculus, not an invitation to infer an unconstrained continuation.

The operator family is procedural and local. The existence of an admissibility symbol does not by itself establish a completed algebra, a physical law, or a global decision procedure.

### 7. Metric and statistical projections

The theory moves from relation to measurement by declaring a projection and its retained invariants. The asymmetry ratio \(\Omega_a\) is a candidate relational measure; it is not automatically a physical ratio.

Statistical projection \(\Leftrightarrow_s\) is allowed only when the outcome set, normalization, and interpretation of the measured quantity are declared. A projected value may summarize a distribution of admissible continuations, but the projection can lose ordering, residue, or orientation information.

Projection-domain equivalence means that two representations preserve a declared invariant set under a declared mapping. It does not mean that the representations are identical in every respect.

### 8. Minimum distinction and regularization

The floor \(\epsilon\) supplies a minimum distinction threshold. It prevents ratios and update rules from being evaluated at an untyped zero boundary. Singularity rebound is modeled as a recursive trigger for renewed deviation rather than as a terminal object.

The regularization role can be summarized as:

\[
D(S_1\mid S_2)\ge\epsilon
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\text{the continuation remains distinguishable within the declared domain}.
\]

This is a conditional model rule. It does not establish that a physical system has the same threshold or that the framework resolves physical singularities.

### 9. Composite directional coupling and realization

The composite coupling chapter combines possibility, history, forward admissibility, and reverse support. The particle-wave operator induction is a projection-level construction: localized selection and distributed continuation are different descriptions of a relational process.

Arbitration and selection convert a set of admissible candidates into a realized continuation. The optimal mismatch principle \(O^*\), selection function \(\mathrm{Sel}_A\), NavT reconciliation, and realization cycle are procedural candidates. Their definitions are useful for bounded models, but they do not constitute an empirical theory without independently recoverable measurements.

### 10. Topology, knots, braids, and geometry

Topology records persistence and connectivity of relational organization. Knot and braid structures represent repeated coupling and closure. The textbook’s biconditional is:

\[
\text{Topology}\ \Leftrightarrow\ \text{Geometry}
\]

within the framework’s co-conditioning projection model. Geometry is not placed beneath topology as a primitive substrate; both are projections of the same recursive process.

Relational basin signatures \(\Sigma_R\) summarize persistent organization. A basin is a condition for possible identity; it is not automatically an object. Closure can make a bounded realization identifiable, but the map from basin condition to identity remains scoped.

### 11. Application projections

The application suffix \(_{app}\) marks a projection domain. Matter, energy, gravity, space, time, curvature, and direction are treated as projected observables or analog structures rather than primitives of the underlying relational process.

The application model therefore preserves a firewall:

\[
\text{RT source relation}
\longrightarrow
\text{declared projection signature}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{physical identity}.
\]

The textbook describes QM-like features such as discrete admissibility transitions, non-commuting trace order, statistical realization, and local closure variance. It describes GR-like features such as topology persistence, large-scale closure continuity, metric-like deformation invariance, and geometry extracted from topology. These are structural signatures and bridge candidates, not derivations of quantum mechanics or general relativity.

## Part II — Bridge material extracted from the textbook

### 12. Orientation–closure bridge

The live orientation–closure bridge connects orientation, admissibility, and closure. It treats an oriented relational condition as the input to a bounded closure procedure. The bridge retains falsification and support conditions and remains a candidate procedural comparison.

Its useful abstraction is:

\[
O\text{-conditioned relation}
\xrightarrow{\delta_a}
\text{admissible continuation}
\xrightarrow{\mathrm{Cl}_x}
\text{bounded closure signature}.
\]

The bridge does not authorize a universal orientation law or a physical identification of the closure signature.

### 13. Projection-domain and QM-like bridge

The QM-like projection is a distinction-weighted domain emphasizing discreteness, exclusion, participation thresholds, and local admissibility. The bridge is analogical and local. A reference-relative map may be written:

\[
M_R:C_{RT}\to V_R,
\qquad
\Pi_R(A\mid E)=\left|\langle M_R(A),M_R(E)\rangle\right|^2.
\]

The squared overlap is a compatibility or realization-strength candidate. It is not probability until normalization, an outcome space, and measurement semantics are supplied. Raw RT conditions are not thereby Hilbert-space states.

### 14. GR-like bridge and triadic closure

The GR-like projection emphasizes distributed orientation, stable topology, large-scale closure continuity, and metric-like deformation invariance. The constraint-geometry model treats the relation as triadic:

\[
CG:=\triangle(RT,QM_{app},GR_{app}).
\]

The triangle is a relational closure among projection signatures. It is not a linear hierarchy and does not prove a common physical theory. Edge metrics such as RT–QM tension are internal comparison metrics.

### 15. Shared source and loss differential

The bridge family keeps a shared source relation while preserving the different information retained by each projection:

\[
\mathrm{shared\ trace}(QM_{like},GR_{like})
\ne
\mathrm{identity}(QM,GR).
\]

Projection-loss differentials record what is retained, lost, or distorted by each representation. Agreement between projections does not recover the source relation, and disagreement does not automatically refute it. Conflicting features must remain visible.

### 16. MTO/OTM and one-to-many/many-to-one bridge

The intake and textbook material describe a general decomposition/reconstruction pattern:

\[
RT_0\xrightarrow{D_x}A_x\xrightarrow{M_y}RT_1,
\qquad A_x\ne RT_0,
\qquad RT_1\ne RT_0\ \text{in general}.
\]

MTO closes admissible aspects into a relational whole; OTM exposes or discovers aspects from a whole. Their proposed recursion is:

\[
\mathrm{OTM}:\text{expose aspects}
\to
\mathrm{MTO}:\text{close aspects}
\to
\text{closed RT as a higher-domain primitive}.
\]

This is a candidate procedural calculus. Lawful aspect discovery, termination, relation-index semantics, type/identity separation, and formal proof obligations remain open.

### 17. Continuity/discreteness bridge

The bridge hypothesis is that continuity and discreteness may be domain-dominated realizations of one process rather than universal competing substances:

\[
P_{D_c}=\text{continuous continuation},
\qquad
P_{D_d}=\text{discrete indexed condition},
\qquad D_c\ne D_d.
\]

The proposal retains domain criteria, the projection operator \(\Pi_D\), a cross-domain invariant, and a lawful coupling operator as unresolved obligations. The claim remains a bounded conceptual hypothesis.

### 21. Provisional intake synthesis

Across the 16 queue entries, the common mathematical themes are:

- a whole RT relation decomposed into typed aspects and reconstructed through bounded closure;
- domain-relative rather than absolute primitivity;
- orientation as a condition of admissible continuation rather than time or motion;
- continuity/discreteness as domain projections;
- topology, geometry, QM-like, and GR-like descriptions as projection signatures;
- MTO/OTM as a candidate one-to-many/many-to-one closure and aspect-discovery calculus; and
- residue, boundary, and identity conditions as information that must remain explicit across projections.

The common unresolved work is formal typing, operator semantics, termination, projection invariants, counterexample construction, and independent validation. The intake corpus supports a structured research agenda at C1; it does not support theorem elevation or external physical conclusions.
