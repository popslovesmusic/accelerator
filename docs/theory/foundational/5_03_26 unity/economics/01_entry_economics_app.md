Distinction Wealth serves as the formal bridge from foundational mismatch organization to the `economics_app` domain.
- **Definition:** The amount of admissible difference a system can preserve, recover, adapt, and productively couple without collapsing future continuation.
- **Translation:** Wealth is not stored physical substance; wealth is preserved admissible organizational capacity.

## 6. Candidate Metrics

To operationalize the domain, the following candidate metrics are defined:

1. **Distinction Inventory (DI):** Raw count of distinguishable mismatch candidates.
   - *Warning:* DI is not wealth until filtered through admissibility and organization.
2. **Admissible Distinction Wealth (ADW):** $\Sigma_D$ structures capable of lawful participation in continuation.
3. **Realized Distinction Wealth (RDW):** Admissible $\Sigma_D$ structures actively producing resilience, adaptation, innovation, recovery, or exchange value.
4. **Distinction Loss (DL):** Collapse, erasure, or unrecoverable narrowing of admissible $\Sigma_D$ structures.
5. **Distinction Recovery (DR):** Ability to restore $\Sigma_D$ structures or regenerate equivalent admissible organization after loss.
6. **Distinction Efficiency (DE):** Effectiveness of converting ADW into RDW.
   - *Form:* $DE = RDW / ADW$
   - *Warning:* Must be paired with scale and resilience to avoid rewarding small, fragile systems.
7. **Distinction Concentration (DC):** Degree to which economic $\Sigma_D$ capacity is concentrated into few fragile pathways.

### Distinction Wealth Index (DWI)
- **Simple Form:** 
  $$DWI = (ADW + RDW + DR) - (DL + DC_{fragility})$$
- **Weighted Form:** 
  $$DWI = w_1(ADW) + w_2(RDW) + w_3(DR) + w_4(DE) - w_5(DL) - w_6(DC_{fragility})$$
- **Governance Warning:** Weights must remain domain-specific until cross-domain validation exists.

## 7. Procedural Economic Laws

1. **Organization Before Wealth:** Mismatch inventory becomes economic wealth only after admissible organization.
2. **Distinction Loss Constraint:** A system cannot increase long-term realized distinction wealth if structurally significant distinction loss persistently exceeds recovery or regeneration.
3. **Fragmentation Limit:** Increasing raw distinction inventory can reduce wealth if it destroys admissibility, coupling, or recovery capacity.
4. **Coupling Productivity Principle:** Economic growth increases when non-equivalent $\Sigma_D$ structures productively couple without collapsing into uniformity.
5. **Resilience Through Redundant Distinction:** Systems with multiple admissible $\Sigma_D$ pathways recover better than systems dependent on concentrated single-path organization.

## 8. Impact Domains

- **poverty:** Collapse or restriction of admissible organizational options. A system may possess resources and still be procedurally poor if recovery, coupling, and $\Sigma_D$ diversity are low.
- **wealth:** Preserved and recoverable admissible organizational capacity. Wealth should be measured by future possibility capacity, not merely stored assets.
- **innovation:** Formation, deformation, or recoupling of $\Sigma_D$ into new admissible organization. Innovation policy should protect exploratory distinction without allowing fragmentation collapse.
- **markets:** Admissible coupling environments among $\Sigma_D$ structures. A healthy market preserves many lawful alternatives while enabling productive interaction.
- **institutions:** High-persistence $\Sigma_D$ structures that stabilize admissibility for future exchange. Institutional decay is loss of organizational signature, not merely loss of personnel or funding.
- **supply_chains:** Coupled $\Sigma_D$ sequences enabling material and procedural continuation. Fragility increases when distinction concentration rises and recovery pathways shrink.
