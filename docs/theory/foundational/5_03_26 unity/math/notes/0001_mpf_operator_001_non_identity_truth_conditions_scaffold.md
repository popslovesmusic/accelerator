# MPF_OPERATOR_001 Non-Identity Truth Conditions Scaffold

## Scope

This note records `<≠>_X` as a provisional candidate operator family for expressing relation with preserved distinction inside the Mono-Process Framework.

This is a research scaffold only. It does not promote theorem status, empirical status, or external-world claims.

## Directly Observed / Defined

- Candidate family template: `A <R>_X B`
- Core candidate form: `A <≠>_X δa(B)`
- Plain reading: `A` remains related to an admissibly updated `B` under context `X`, while distinction between `A` and `B` remains nonzero.
- Intended gap filled: relation can be stated without collapsing into identity.

## Inferred Inside Framework

The candidate is intended to sit between two already used ideas:

1. `D(A|B) > 0`
2. `A ⇔_X B`

The proposed reading is:

1. Distinction exists.
2. Distinction participates in relation.
3. Relation does not erase distinction.

Within that restricted reading, `<≠>_X` is a candidate non-collapse relation marker rather than a replacement for `⇔_X` or `⇔_R`.

## Relation to Existing MPF Operators

- `D(A|B) > 0` states distinguishability.
- `A ⇔_X B` states coupling or admissible participation.
- `A <≠>_X B` would add the claim that coupling does not imply identity collapse.
- `A ⇔_R B` remains the residue-conditioned stabilization form and should not be replaced by `<≠>_X`.

## Minimum Candidate Truth Conditions

The following are proposed as the minimum local conditions to investigate, not settled law:

1. `D(A|B) > 0` remains satisfied in the declared context `X`.
2. A relation between `A` and `B` is already declared or derivable, typically by `A ⇔_X B`.
3. The relation preserves typed role distinction, so `A` and `B` do not collapse into the same operational slot under `X`.
4. Any admissible update applied to `B` does not force `A = B` as a primitive or observational identity claim.

Compact candidate reading:

`A <≠>_X B` is admissible only if relation and distinction co-survive under context `X`.

## Counterexample Directions

The candidate should fail or remain undefined when:

1. `D(A|B) -> 0` under the same declared context.
2. The only available reading of the relation is strict identity.
3. Context `X` is untyped or changes across the statement without declaration.
4. A projection confuses observable similarity with identity.

## Example Readings

### Graphics

- `Sprite <≠>_obs Entity`
- Restricted reading: the observed sprite is related to the entity but is not identical to the entity's deeper orientation-conditioned identity trace.

### BFT / organism framing

- `Organism <≠>_bio Subsystem`
- Restricted reading: biological coupling does not by itself erase subsystem distinction.

### Observer systems

- `Observer <≠>_obs Observed`
- Restricted reading: observation relation does not justify observer-observed identity collapse.

## External Resemblance

This candidate resembles distinction-preserving relation notation used in typed semantics, model theory, category-flavored relation languages, and observer/projection discussions.

That resemblance is analogy only. This note does not claim equivalence to those external formalisms.

## What It Does Not Prove

- It does not prove a full algebra for `<≠>_X`.
- It does not prove symmetry, transitivity, commutativity, or compositional closure.
- It does not prove any graphics engine, biological, or observer result.
- It does not prove that identity "lives deeper than pixels" outside the scoped model language used here.

## Failure Modes / Uncertainty

- The operator may be redundant if `⇔_X` plus explicit distinction constraints already suffice.
- The operator may fragment the algebra if context typing is underspecified.
- The operator may collapse into a slogan unless its truth conditions and failure conditions are explicit.
- The relation may need directional typing, so `A <≠>_X B` may differ from `B <≠>_X A`.
