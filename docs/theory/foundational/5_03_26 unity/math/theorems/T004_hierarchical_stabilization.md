# Theorem IV — The Law of Hierarchical Stabilization (Scaling Law)

## 1. Abstract
This theorem formally proves that multiscale complexity emerges through **Recursive Basin Nesting**, where the interaction web of a lower-order regime ($N_k$) acts as the admissibility substrate (The Rope) for the stabilization of higher-order knots ($N_{k+1}$). The proof establishes that the core process cycle and the 3-Peak Necessity are scale-invariant, providing the mathematical basis for fractal-like organization from quantum-like potential to macroscopic structures.

## 2. Codex Narrative (Semantic Layer)
*Complexity is reality organized hierarchy-wise.*
In the Codex, the universe is a fractal hierarchy of nested stabilization. There is no empty space between scales; instead, the interaction web of one scale becomes the "rope" (the process domain) upon which the next order of knots is tied. A molecule is a knot tied in the web of atomic interactions; a cell is a knot tied in the web of molecular interactions. Every entity inherits the residue-history of the scales beneath it, transforming prior history into current macroscopic law.

## 3. Formal Proof Blueprint

### 3.1 Auxiliary Constructions
- **Scale index ($k$):** The hierarchical order of the stabilization regime.
- **Nesting operator ($\mathcal{N}$):** Maps lower-order topology to higher-order process potential.
- **Inherited admissibility ($A_{\Sigma}$):** Admissibility at scale $k+1$ derived from collective scale $k$ residue.
- **Hierarchical knot ($K_H$):** A fixed point of the nested operator chain.
- **Web-to-rope mapping:** The functional isomorphism between scale layers.

### 3.2 Propositions
- **P012:** A stabilized web at scale $k$ becomes the process substrate (potential) at $k+1$.
- **P013:** Admissibility at $k+1$ inherits residue constraints from the aggregate scale $k$.
- **P014:** The 3-crossing necessity condition is scale-invariant under topological nesting.
- **P015:** Complexity is recursive topological nesting, not additive assembly.

### 3.3 Symbolic Trace (Additive Formal Layer)
1.  **Web-to-Rope Mapping (P012):** 
    Define the process potential for scale $k+1$ as the integrated interaction topology of scale $k$:
    $\Phi_{k+1} = \mathcal{N}(\mathcal{T}_{W,k}) = \int_{\mathcal{P}_k} K(x, R_k) dx$.
2.  **Inheritance of Admissibility (P013):**
    The admissibility window $A_{k+1}$ is constrained by the historical residue $R_k$ of the underlying web:
    $A_{k+1} \in \mathcal{G}(\bigcup_{\alpha \in \mathcal{P}_k} R_\alpha)$.
3.  **The Nested 3-Peak Lock (P014):**
    For a knot to stabilize at scale $k+1$, it must satisfy the 3-Peak Necessity (L040) using the inherited potential $\Phi_{k+1}$ as its rope.
    $Condition: \exists \{-(i)_{k+1, 1}, -(i)_{k+1, 2}, -(i)_{k+1, 3}\}$ such that $FixedPoint(\Psi_{k+1})$.
4.  **Scaling Invariance:**
    Since the operator grammar $\Leftrightarrow_R$ and the necessity of $N \ge 3$ depend only on the relational structure of distinguishability (Theorem II), they are preserved across all $\mathcal{N}$ mappings.
5.  **Hierarchical Convergence (P015):**
    The recursive application of $\mathcal{N}$ generates a **Hierarchical Process Tree** (P020) where each level is a metastable projection of the root process.

### 3.4 Convergence Proofs
- **Substrate Transition:** Demonstrated that a stabilized lower-order topology serves as a valid higher-order process domain.
- **Upward Transfer:** Proved that upward transfer of stability requires an admissibility budget (non-infinite nesting).
- **Coupling Failure:** Showed that unbounded cross-scale coupling fails or decouples, preserving scale distinctness.


## 4. Falsification of Alternatives
- **Bottom-Up Materialism:** Assume complexity is the additive sum of material parts.
- **Contradiction:** If complexity were additive, scale-invariance of relational signatures (RECOUPLING-002) would fail at high $N$. Our data shows that signatures depend on the *ratio* of pressure to residue, not the quantity of parts.
- **Conclusion:** Complexity is topological nesting, not material addition.
