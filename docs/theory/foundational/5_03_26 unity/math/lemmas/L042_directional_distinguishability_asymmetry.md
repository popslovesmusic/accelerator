# L042 — Directional Distinguishability Asymmetry ($\Delta D_R$)

## Statement
Relational distinguishability between two process states $S_1$ and $S_2$ is inherently asymmetric when evaluated from the perspective of an oriented admissibility regime. Formally: $D(S_1 \mid S_2) \neq D(S_2 \mid S_1)$. The non-zero difference $\Delta D_R = D(S_1 \mid S_2) - D(S_2 \mid S_1)$ is the irreducible generator of emergent dominant orientation ($-(i)_{Dom}$).

## Dependencies
- Definitions: `directional_distinguishability`, `distinguishability_asymmetry`, `dominant_orientation`
- Assumptions: Relation is primary; absolute symmetry is an unstable limit.
- Prior lemmas: L034 (Generalized Operator Grammar), L040 (The Triangle Law)

## Proof (or Proof sketch)
1. Let $S_1$ and $S_2$ be two nodes in a coupled process basin.
2. In any non-null state, relational mismatch $\varepsilon$ persists (Theorem I).
3. Evaluation of distinguishability is path-dependent and conditioned by local residue $R$.
4. Because the process is recursive, the transition $S_1 \to S_2$ and $S_2 \to S_1$ encounter different historical deformations of the admissibility window.
5. This path-dependence induces asymmetry in the resolvable mismatch: $D(S_1 \mid S_2) \neq D(S_2 \mid S_1)$.
6. The resulting $\Delta D_R$ creates a directional pressure that "breaks" the local symmetry.
7. This pressure forces the selection of a preferred orientation, resulting in the emergent reference $-(i)_{Dom}$.
8. Therefore, orientational dominance is not a primitive but a consequence of unresolved directional distinguishability.
