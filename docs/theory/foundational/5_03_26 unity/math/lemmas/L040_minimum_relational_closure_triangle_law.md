# L040 — Minimum Relational Closure (The Triangle Law)

## Statement
Stable relational distinguishability ($D(S_1 \parallel S_2) > 0$) cannot be maintained in a 1st-order binary opposition. Structural persistence requires a minimum 3rd-order recursive loop (The Triangle) to provide the necessary orientational reinforcement for residue inscription. The triangle is the minimum closure topology for a self-maintaining process basin.

## Dependencies
- Definitions: `distinguishability_flux`, `triangle_closure`, `process_basin`
- Assumptions: Binary relations are subject to symmetry collapse; stabilization requires circulating reinforcement.
- Prior lemmas: L035 (Relational Distinguishability Threshold), L037 (Entity as Stabilized Continuation Mode)

## Proof (or Proof sketch)
1. Let $A$ and $B$ be two process states in a binary relation.
2. In a purely binary system, any update $\Delta$ that distinguishes $A$ from $B$ lacks a third reference to anchor the orientation operator $-(i)$.
3. Without a 3rd-order anchor, the orientational lock is unstable and tends toward symmetry collapse ($D \to 0$), dissolving the distinction.
4. Introduce a third node $C$, forming a triangle $(A, B, C)$.
5. Distinguishability flows along the edges: $D_{AB}, D_{BC}, D_{CA}$.
6. This recursive circulation allows residue $R$ to accumulate through mutual reinforcement: the distinction $A-B$ is preserved by its relationship to $C$.
7. Thus, the triangle is the simplest manifold capable of maintaining a non-zero distinguishability floor $\theta$ against dissipative symmetry pressure.
