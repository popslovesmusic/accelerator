# Lemma L121 — Emergence of Distinction from Primitive Relational Condition

## 1. Statement
Distinction is not a primitive ontological element of the Mono-Process Framework. Instead, admissible distinction emerges co-dependently from a primitive relational condition `(*|*)` through the symmetric relational biconditional relation `<-S->`. Formally:
\[
(*|*) \Leftrightarrow_S \delta_a(\mathcal{E} \neq 0)
\]
This prevents substrate reification and anchors distinction to local admissibility constraints.

## 3. Proof Sketch
1.  **Reductio ad Absurdum:** Assume distinction $\mathcal{E} \neq 0$ is primitive. Under classical distinction theories, a primitive distinction requires a pre-existing background manifold or coordinate container space to distinguish *where* or *what* is distinct. This introduces an unvalidated primitive container space, violating the Ontological Compression Constraint (Global Governance) and the Topology-Geometry Biconditional (L045).
2.  **Relational Primacy:** By defining the primitive relational condition `(*|*)` as the pre-distinction locus, we represent purely co-present constraints prior to division.
3.  **Biconditional Coupling:** The symmetric biconditional $\Leftrightarrow_S$ establishes mutual necessity. The existence of distinction $\mathcal{E} \neq 0$ occurs if and only if it is filtered by the admissibility operator $\delta_a$, which excludes incompatible configurations.
Therefore, distinction emerges as an admissible existential condition from the primitive relational condition. $\blacksquare$
