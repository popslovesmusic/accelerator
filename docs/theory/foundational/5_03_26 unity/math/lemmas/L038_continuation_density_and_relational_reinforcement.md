# L038 — Continuation Density and Relational Reinforcement

## Statement
The apparent "density" of a process domain is not a measure of material quantity, but is proportional to the local frequency of relational reinforcement events ($ \Delta \succcurlyeq \theta $). High-density regions (continuation filaments) correspond to stable orientational corridors with high reinforcement rates, while low-density regions (continuation voids) correspond to domains of unresolved distinction or inadmissible continuation.

## Dependencies
- Definitions: `continuation_density`, `reinforcement_rate`, `theta_crossing`
- Assumptions: Continuity is procedural; density is an emergent operational metric.
- Prior lemmas: L035 (Relational Distinguishability Threshold), L037 (Entity as Stabilized Continuation Mode)

## Proof (or Proof sketch)
1. Let $\rho(x)$ be the local continuation density at $x$.
2. Let $f_\theta(x, \Delta t)$ be the frequency of threshold-crossing selection events in the interval $\Delta t$.
3. By L036, each selection event "ratchets" the admissibility manifold, increasing the probability of future selection in the same orientational frame.
4. Regions with high $f_\theta$ create deep continuation channels (reinforcement), which present to an observer as "dense" or "material-like" structures.
5. Regions with $f_\theta \approx 0$ lack relational stabilization and remain in a state of unresolved coherence (void).
6. Thus, $\rho(x) \propto f_\theta(x)$ is the governing relation for emergent spatial topology.
