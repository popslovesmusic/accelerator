# L047 — Stratified Process Ontology

## Statement
The Mono-Process Framework organizes existence into a hierarchical sequence of selective filtration stages: **Admissibility** $\to$ **Realization** $\to$ **Actualization** $\to$ **Persistence**. This stratification prevents ontological collapse and defines the rarity of stable identity within the process.

## Definitions of Strata
1.  **Admissibility:** The state of being capable of participation (mismatch $\varepsilon \neq 0$).
2.  **Realization:** The state of being locally instantiated (coupled distinction).
3.  **Actualization:** The state of being recursively selected for continuation (ratchet event).
4.  **Persistence:** The state of stabilized recursive continuation (basin/knot).

## Proof (or Proof sketch)
1. Within this framework, being "admissible" does not guarantee persistence.
2. The vast majority of distinguishability events are transient and fail to achieve recursive closure.
3. Persistence requires the simultaneous satisfaction of multiple constraints: triadic closure (L040), functional partitioning (L043), and residue-conditioned stability.
4. By stratifying the ontology, we can model the transition from potential to stabilized identity as a multi-stage filtering process.
5. This hierarchy is consistent with the observed rarity of stable "particles" or "objects" relative to the background process flux.
