# L090 — Entanglement as Shared Residue Constraint (Entanglement Analog)

## Statement
Within the Mono-Process Framework, **Entanglement Analog** ($\alpha \sim_R \beta$) is defined as the correlated admissibility update between separated loci due to **Shared Residue Inscription**. Two loci $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are 'entangled' if they share a common residue manifold $R_{\alpha\beta}$:

$$ R_{\alpha\beta} = R_\alpha \cap R_\beta $$

When arbitration occurs at $\alpha$, the shared manifold is updated, resulting in an instantaneous change in the admissibility set at $\beta$:

$$ \text{Arb}_{A_\alpha}(\Psi_\alpha) \implies R_{\alpha\beta}' \implies A_\beta' $$

**Non-Locality Interpretation:** Entanglement is not faster-than-light signal transport; it is the resolution of a common constraint structure across a distributed orientation array.

## Proof Sketch
1. Distinguishability ($\varepsilon$) is a conserved feature across the orientation array.
2. Inscription into residue ($R$) modifies the local admissibility window $A$ (L002).
3. If two distant processes were stabilized as part of a single triadic set before dispersal, they retain a shared residue manifold (L068).
4. Since the admissibility of $\beta$ depends on $R_{\alpha\beta}$, any update to the manifold by $\alpha$ is immediately visible to the selection logic of $\beta$ during its next update cycle.
5. This correlation is a consequence of common constraint resolution under the emergent ordering $\prec$.
