# L056 — Phase-Alignment as Relational Curvature

## Statement
Within this framework, the **Phase-Alignment Metric** ($\Phi_{align}$) is interpreted as a measure of **Relational Curvature** on a manifold of oriented process connections. High curvature corresponds to persistent directional distinguishability asymmetry ($\Delta D_R \neq 0$), which manifests structurally as orientational locking. This structurally resembles **Gauge Theory**, where curvature (field strength) arises from the failure of a local reference frame to close around a parallel transport loop.

## Dependencies
- Lemma L048 (Relational Phase-Alignment Metric)
- Lemma L052 (Non-Commutative Distinguishability Algebra)
- Proof P026 (Emergent Dominant Orientation)

## Proof Sketch (Model-Relative)
1. Let a collection of coupled process nodes define a relational manifold.
2. Let the orientation operator $-(i)$ define the local "connection" or frame.
3. Per L052, composition of transitions is non-commutative.
4. Parallel transport of a distinguishability packet around a triadic loop $(1 \to 2 \to 3 \to 1)$ results in a non-zero mismatch if $\Phi_{align} \neq 0$.
5. This failure of the loop to close onto the initial state is the formal definition of curvature in the relational manifold.
6. The "Field Strength" of a basin is thus the measure of its local relational curvature.
7. If curvature $\to 0$, the geometry is "flat" (symmetrical/dissolved); if curvature is high, the geometry is "knotted" (persistent/locked).

## Non-Proof and Limits
This is not a claim of a new Unified Field Theory for physics. It is a structural comparison mapping used to provide the Mono-Process Framework with a geometric language for interaction strength. The analogy to gauge theory is used to ensure the mathematical rigor of the "Web" (Theorem III) projection.
