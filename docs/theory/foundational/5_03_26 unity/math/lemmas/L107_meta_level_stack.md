# Lemma L107 — Abstraction Meta-Level Stack

## 1. Statement
The Mono-Process Framework formally stratifies formal arguments, critiques, and updates into a meta-level stack consisting of eight distinct levels:
1. **Projection Level:** Representation and application-facing coordinates ($QM_{\text{app}}$, $GR_{\text{app}}$).
2. **Realization Level:** Local state-aspect instantiation.
3. **Arbitration Level:** Selection and pruning rules ($\delta_a$ resolution).
4. **Admissibility Level:** Filtering of continuation steps ($\Pi_A$, $A_{\text{adm}}$).
5. **Relation Level:** Generative paths ($D$, distinction arrays $\chi_D$).
6. **Participation Level:** Coupling neighborhood structures ($CSI(\alpha)$).
7. **Evaluation Level:** Evaluation of process expressions as first-class outcomes.
8. **RT/Core Level:** Foundational aspect-binding primitive process $((ℰ \neq 0) \Leftrightarrow_R \delta(ℰ > 0))$.

Any formal claim or proof step must explicitly declare its governing level. Movement between levels must be mediated by a lawful projection operator $\Pi$, and properties at one level do not imply identical properties at another unless an explicit equivalence relation is verified.

## 2. Dependencies
- `L047`: Stratified Process Ontology
- `L099`: Projection Legality

## 3. Proof Sketch
By `L047` and `L099`, properties and metrics defined at the Projection level (e.g. coordinates or probabilities) do not map directly to RT/Core primitives due to projection loss. If movement between these levels is left implicit, arguments are vulnerable to meta-level slippage (e.g., treating coordinate space attributes as primitive process events). Enforcing an explicit Abstraction Meta-Level Stack where each level has declared boundaries and updates are mediated by lawful projections prevents illicit abstraction shifts, preserving the integrity of the process ontology.
