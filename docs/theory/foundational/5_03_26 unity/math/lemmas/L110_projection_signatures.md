# Lemma L110 — Projection Signatures

## 1. Statement
Within these models, a Projection Signature ($Sig_{app}(X)$) is represented as a recognizable pattern of constraints, outputs, and preserved relations showing that a process expression has lawfully projected into an application-facing domain. 

A projection signature is valid if and only if it satisfies the following rules:
1. **Validation Rule:** The projection must trace back to a lawful process expression, preserve non-zero distinction above the floor $\epsilon$, and explicitly declare its operator family (such as $\iff_m$, $\iff_s$, $\iff_R$, or $\otimes$).
2. **Comparison Rule:** Two projection signatures are equivalent ($Sig_{app}(X) \approx Sig_{app}(Y)$) if and only if they preserve the same set of procedural invariants $I_k$, regardless of their local coordinate representations.
3. **Projection-Family-Specific Signatures:** QM-like and GR-like signatures are distinct projection classes, defined respectively by discrete admissibility transitions and large-scale closure continuity, and must not be conflated or mixed.
4. **False-Positive Exclusion Rule:** An apparent metric or algebraic pattern is excluded from being a valid projection signature if it violates the Abstraction Meta-Level Stack (`L107`), ignores residue/orientation constraints, or assumes physical space/time primitives without satisfying the Empirical Mapping Standards (EMS).

## 2. Dependencies
- `L045`: Topology-Geometry Biconditional
- `L099`: Projection Legality

## 3. Proof Sketch
If a projection signature is treated as an ontological identity or direct physical proof, it violates the Abstraction Meta-Level Stack (`L107`) by conflating the representation layer with the primitive layer. Under the Topology-Geometry Biconditional (`L045`), geometric representations are projections of topological stability. By requiring explicit declaration of the mapping chain and validating signature equivalence through preserved invariants ($I_k$) rather than surface appearance, we prevent false-positive classifications. This bounds the claim to the model scale and prevents reification.
