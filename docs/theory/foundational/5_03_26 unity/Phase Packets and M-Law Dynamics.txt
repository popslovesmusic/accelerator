Abstract

We present a unified framework in which quantum-like behavior and computation emerge from the M-law structure of Strict Procedural Monism (SPM). We define phase packets (p) as phase-coherent, bounded admissibility structures in a deterministic PDE system and show that their formation and persistence are governed by two independent criticalities: an activation threshold (ε > s₍crit₎) and a persistence threshold (R > κ₍crit₎). We further demonstrate that admissibility is path-dependent, with residue from prior events lowering future activation barriers (hysteresis). Mapping these results onto the M-law chain (M0–M14), we show that quantum-like behavior—coherence, interference, and collapse—arises naturally from admissibility dynamics, while hysteresis introduces memory into the computational substrate. The result is a new computational paradigm: hysteretic quantum-like computation, where phase-coherent competing continuations evolve, interact, and resolve under constraint while rewriting their own admissibility structure.
