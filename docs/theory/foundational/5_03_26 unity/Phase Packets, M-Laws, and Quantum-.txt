Phase Packets, M-Laws, and Quantum-Like Computation

A Law-Based Derivation of Quantum-Like Behavior in Deterministic Systems

Abstract

We present a formal mapping between the M-law framework of Strict Procedural Monism (SPM) and quantum-like behavior, with a focus on phase packets as candidate computational primitives. We define “quantum-like” operationally as the coexistence of coherent competing continuations whose relative phase affects outcomes, whose interactions exhibit interference, and which resolve under constraint into discrete selections. We show that these properties emerge naturally from the M-law chain (M0–M14), particularly through activation (M2), admissibility filtering (M7), residue persistence (M6, M8), and collapse (M13). The biconditional master relation (ℰ ≠ 0) ⇔ δ(ℰ > 0) provides a unified interpretation of pre-selection (quantum-like) and post-selection (classical/geometric) regimes. We conclude that quantum-like computation can be interpreted as controlled manipulation of phase-coherent admissibility structures rather than requiring fundamentally quantum substrates.

1. Introduction

Quantum computation relies on three core phenomena:

Coherence: preservation of phase relations between states
Interference: phase-dependent constructive/destructive outcomes
Collapse/measurement: selection of a single outcome

In standard physics, coherence is defined as maintaining a well-defined phase relationship between components of a system , and interference arises when phase relationships determine amplification or cancellation .

We propose that these behaviors are not exclusive to quantum substrates but emerge from a deeper structural process described by the M-laws.
