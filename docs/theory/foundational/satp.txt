# Harmonic Scale-Anchored Transport Theory

## A Research and Testing Framework within SPM

## 1. Core Thesis

Reality is treated as one underlying process that projects into two coupled domains:

1. **Continuous domain**: oscillatory, phase-bearing, field-like structure.
2. **Discrete domain**: stabilized basins formed by phase-locked harmonic regimes.

In this model, **transport is not primitive motion through space**. Transport is the coherent transition of a stabilized identity-pattern from one harmonic basin to another while maintaining phase continuity.

> Scale-anchored transport occurs when a phase-locked structure shifts between admissible harmonic basins without losing coherence.

This extends the SPM claim that phenomena are coherent oscillatory structures stabilized by phase-locking under constraint. 

---

## 2. Foundational Assumptions

### A. No zero state

There is no perfect null condition:

[
\varepsilon \neq 0
]

Mismatch is always present. This means reality is never static; it is always in active deviation.

### B. Stability is oscillatory, not static

A stable phenomenon is not motionless. It is a bounded oscillatory pattern:

[
\text{Stable} \equiv \text{bounded phase-locked oscillatory regime}
]

This follows directly from the existing SPM note: stability is sustained self-consistent oscillation under constraint. 

### C. Basins are harmonic attractors

A basin is defined as a persistent phase-locked harmonic regime.

[
B_i = \text{PhaseLock}(H_i, C)
]

Where:

* (B_i) = basin identity
* (H_i) = harmonic mode or mode-family
* (C) = admissibility constraint structure

So basin identity is not merely location. It is a stable harmonic signature.

---

## 3. Continuous and Discrete Projection

The “one” projects into two domains:

### Continuous projection

A phase-bearing oscillatory substrate:

[
\Psi(x) = A(x)e^{i\phi(x)}
]

This represents smooth variation, relational phase, and possible continuation.

### Discrete projection

Stable harmonic basins:

[
{B_1, B_2, B_3, ...}
]

These are not separate substances. They are stabilized attractors inside the continuous phase structure.

Thus:

[
\text{Discrete identity} = \text{stable harmonic closure within continuous phase}
]

## 4. Scale as Harmonic Regime

Scale is not raw size. In SPM, scale is meaningful only relative to the local orientation basin. The warp/anchored-scale note states that scale is deviation and persistence measured relative to the locally stabilized orientation operator. 

So define scale as:

[
\sigma(B_i) = d(H_i, \omega_t)
]

Where:

* (\sigma(B_i)) = anchored scale of basin (B_i)
* (H_i) = harmonic signature of basin
* (\omega_t) = local orientation basin
* (d) = mismatch/deviation metric

A basin is “larger” or “higher-scale” not because it occupies more space, but because its harmonic persistence deviates more strongly or broadly from the local orientation baseline.

---

## 5. Transport as Harmonic Transition

Ordinary motion says:

[
x_1 \rightarrow x_2
]

This theory replaces that with:

[
B_i \rightarrow B_j
]

Transport is a transition between harmonic basins.

But not every transition counts as transport. A valid transport event requires preserved identity-coherence:

[
\Delta \phi_{ij} \rightarrow \text{bounded}
]

That is:

> Transport succeeds only if phase relation remains bounded during basin transition.

If coherence fails, the structure does not transport; it decoheres, collapses, or re-locks locally.

---

## 6. Harmonic Scale-Anchored Transport

A stronger form of transport occurs when the system shifts between basins of different anchored scale:

[
B_i(\sigma_i) \rightarrow B_j(\sigma_j)
]

where:

[
\sigma_i \neq \sigma_j
]

This is **scale-anchored transport**.

It means:

> The system is not merely changing position; it is changing harmonic scale-regime while maintaining identity coherence.

This may be the SPM-compatible interpretation of “warp-like” transport: not motion through spacetime, but forced coherent transition into a nonlocal or non-natural harmonic basin.

The uploaded warp paper already frames warp transport as forced anchored-scale misalignment maintained as a corridor against the local orientation basin.

A minimal research test:

1. Create a multi-channel oscillatory system.
2. Allow phase-locked harmonic basins to form.
3. Identify basin signatures (S_B).
4. Define local orientation basin (\omega_t).
5. Inject controlled harmonic forcing.
6. Attempt transition:

[

7. Measure whether phase coherence persists.
8. Remove forcing.
9. Observe whether the system:

Expected success signature:

[
K_H \text{ remains high}, \quad T_{ij} \text{ remains bounded}, \quad \Delta \theta_r \text{ avoids catastrophic flip}
]

Expected failure signature:

[
K_H \downarrow,\quad C(r,t)\downarrow,\quad |\Delta \theta_r|\uparrow

## 10. Canonical Statement

> Harmonic scale-anchored transport is the coherent transition of a phase-locked identity-pattern between admissible harmonic basins of differing anchored scale. Basins are discrete stabilized projections of an underlying continuous oscillatory domain. Transport succeeds when phase coherence is preserved across basin transition; it fails when harmonic relation collapses and the system re-synchronizes to the local orientation basin.

---

## 11. Research Question

The central testable question is:

> Can a phase-locked oscillatory structure be forced from one harmonic basin into another while preserving its phase identity signature?

If yes, transport can be modeled as coherent harmonic transition.

If no, the structure collapses into local re-locking rather than transport.
