Theoretical Framework: Relational OperationalismThe paper moves away from seeking an absolute "smallest unit" of reality (ontological minimality) and instead establishes a framework based on operational detectability.Relational Baseline ("The Floor"): The theory posits that detection is not a measurement against a static vacuum, but a divergence from a dynamic baseline continuation ($ε_0$). In this deterministic system, the "floor" is the admissible evolution of the PDE without any injected deviation.The Phase Packet $(p)$ as an Operational Object: Rather than a discrete primitive or "particle," a phase packet is theoretically defined as a bounded, phase-coherent continuation structure that emerges relative to the baseline. Any discrete-like behavior is viewed as emergent from stability constraints rather than an inherent property.Protocol-Bound Detectability: The theory asserts that "minimal units" are actually functions of the measurement protocol, including numerical tolerance ($\eta$), persistence windows ($T_p$), and solver discretization.2. Core HypothesesPrimary Research HypothesisThe central inquiry of the paper is whether a reliably distinguishable "smallest injected deviation" exists for a fixed model and measurement protocol.The Null Result Hypothesis: The implicit hypothesis tested throughout the scans is that there might be a "mixed detection regime" or an intrinsic lower cutoff where an injected deviation becomes indistinguishable from the baseline.Empirical Findings (Hypothesis Testing)The results of the high-resolution scans led to the following empirical conclusions:Absence of an Intrinsic Cutoff: The hypothesis of an intrinsic lower detectability bound was not supported within tested precision.Reliability Hypothesis: The researchers hypothesized that if an amplitude ($\delta$) yields a detection probability $P_{detect}(\delta) \geq 0.95$, it constitutes a reliable detection.Formal Statement of ResultsUnder the current detector settings ($\eta = 10^{-16}$, $T_p = 50$):$$\Delta\phi_{min,emp} < 10^{-16}$$This indicates that no intrinsic lower detectability bound was observed; the "limit" is currently defined by the resolution of the measurement protocol rather than the physics of the system.
