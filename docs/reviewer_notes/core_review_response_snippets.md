# Reviewer Response Snippet Library

## 1. Purpose
This document provides a library of standard, governance-approved responses to common reviewer misunderstandings, ensuring consistent and accurate communication of the framework's core principles.

---
### **Scenario: "This looks like you're trying to create a new physics."**
**Response Snippet:**
> Thank you for the feedback. To clarify, the Mono-Process Framework is a formal system for modeling process admissibility and continuation; it makes no claims about physical reality. All simulations and mathematical structures are evaluated based on their internal consistency within the framework's own rules, not on their correspondence to physical phenomena. The project's governance layer strictly prohibits physics-based claims.

---
### **Scenario: "Your core expression is just a tautology / not a real equation."**
**Response Snippet:**
> We appreciate the observation, as it highlights a key feature of the framework. The core expression, (ℰ≠0) ⇔_R δ(ℰ>0), is not intended as an equation to be solved, but as a formal declaration of a process principle: that distinguishability and continuation are inseparable, recursively-bound aspects of a single process. The symbol ⇔_R denotes this "recursive aspect-binding," not ordinary logical or algebraic equality. The framework then explores the stabilized structures that derive from this principle.

---
### **Scenario: "Your results are just artifacts of your simulation / topology / geometry."**
**Response Snippet:**
> This is a crucial point. The framework's doctrine explicitly states that topology and geometry are *not* primitive substrates but are themselves **derived projections** of the underlying process dynamics. Our methodology requires that any supported claim must be verified across multiple, independent mechanism classes (e.g., PDE, stochastic, agent-based) precisely to ensure the observed phenomena are true invariants of the core process, not artifacts of a single implementation.

---
### **Scenario: "The framework seems overly complex."**
**Response Snippet:**
> The framework is built bottom-up from a single, simple core principle: distinguishability and continuation are inseparable aspects of one process. The apparent complexity arises from the rich variety of stabilized structures (basins, thresholds, cascades) that can emerge as projections of this single recursive rule under different conditions. The purpose of the many laws and registries is to govern these derived structures with high rigor, ensuring they always trace back to the simple process-first foundation.
