# Plain-Language Core Expression Explanation

## 1. Purpose
This document provides a mandatory, non-technical explanation of the core expression, **(ℰ≠0) ⇔_R δ(ℰ>0)**, suitable for inclusion in abstracts, introductions, and reviewer orientation notes.

## 2. The Core Idea: One Process
The Mono-Process Framework is built on a single, simple idea: **to exist is to continue, and to continue is to be distinguishable from what you are not.**

## 3. Deconstructing the Expression

- **(ℰ≠0) — "Something is different."**
  This part represents any kind of distinction, difference, mismatch, or pressure. It's the "reason" anything happens at all. Without some kind of difference, there is only static uniformity.

- **δ(ℰ>0) — "Something happens next."**
  This part represents continuation, transition, or the selection of a next state. It's the "action" that the process takes in response to a difference.

- **⇔_R — "Are two sides of the same coin, remembered."**
  This is the most important part. It's not an equals sign. It means that the "difference" and the "action" are inseparable aspects of a single process, recursively bound together by the memory of past actions (Residue, R). You can't have one without the other. The history of past actions shapes what actions are possible now.

## 4. The Principle in Plain Language
**The framework's central claim is that distinguishability and continuation are inseparable aspects of one recursive process.** Everything else in the framework—all the complex-looking math, geometry, and topology—is a description of the *stabilized patterns* that emerge from this single, simple, recursive rule.
