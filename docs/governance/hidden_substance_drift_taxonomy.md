# Hidden Substance Drift Taxonomy

## 1. Purpose
This document catalogs common ways that primitive substance assumptions—the idea of pre-existing "things"—can accidentally re-enter the Mono-Process Framework, thereby violating its process-first ontology. It serves as a diagnostic checklist for the `validate_process_first_ontology.py` script.

## 2. Taxonomy of Ontological Drift

### 2.1 Geometry as Substrate
- **Drift:** Describing manifolds, basins, or ridges as pre-existing "landscapes" on which the process runs.
- **Correction:** Geometry is a stabilized projection of process history and admissibility constraints. It is an *effect*, not a container.

### 2.2 Topology as Container
- **Drift:** Treating topology (e.g., a grid, a graph) as a fundamental, fixed background that "contains" the process.
- **Correction:** Topology is a projection of the stabilized *accessibility graph* between process loci. It is a representation of admissible continuation paths, not a static structure.

### 2.3 Operator as Entity
- **Drift:** Referring to operators (δ, Π_A, NavT) as independent agents or entities that "act upon" the system.
- **Correction:** Operators are formal descriptions of the different *aspects* of the single, inseparable process relation.

### 2.4 Memory as Substance
- **Drift:** Describing Residue (R) as a substance that is "stored" or "held" in a location.
- **Correction:** Residue is the active, history-driven conditioning of future admissibility. It is a dynamic constraint, not a stored thing.

### 2.5 Identity as Object Permanence
- **Drift:** Equating the persistence of a channel or structure with the static, enduring identity of a classical object.
- **Correction:** Identity is operational continuity—the ability of a process pattern to successfully continue itself through change. It is a verb, not a noun.

### 2.6 Observer as External Agent
- **Drift:** Framing observation, measurement, or reconstruction as the act of a privileged, external entity looking "into" the system.
- **Correction:** Observation is a local process projection. It is an admissible interaction that is itself part of the process, subject to the same rules of admissibility and finitude.
