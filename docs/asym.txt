

    
    TECHNICAL NOTE

    
    Asymmetry Ratio, Orientation Selection, and Candidate Induction Pathways

    
    Status

    Exploratory mathematical note.

    
    
    Purpose

    To document the emergence of the asymmetry-ratio concept and its potential role as a bridge between relational distinction and orientation selection within the Mono-Process mathematical program.

    

    
    
    
    
    1. Starting Point

    The existing relational asymmetry statement is:

    D(S1|S2) ≠ D(S2|S1)

    where:

    D(S1|S2)

    represents directed distinction from S1 toward S2.

    The statement asserts that directed distinction is generally non-symmetric.

    This relation has previously functioned primarily as a descriptive expression.

    

    
    
    2. Ratio Construction

    Define:

    D(S1|S2) = x

D(S2|S1) = z

    and introduce the candidate asymmetry ratio:

    Ω_a = x/z

    or equivalently:

    Ω_a =
D(S1|S2)
─────────
D(S2|S1)

    

    
    
    3. Immediate Interpretation

    Under ordinary arithmetic:

    Ω_a = 1

    iff

    D(S1|S2) = D(S2|S1)

    This is a symmetry condition.

    Likewise:

    Ω_a > 1

    indicates dominance of:

    D(S1|S2)

    while:

    Ω_a < 1

    indicates dominance of:

    D(S2|S1)

    At this stage Ω_a functions only as a measurement.

    No generative role has yet been established.

    

    
    
    4. Critical Insight

    The discussion revealed a distinction between:

    
    Measurement

    D(S1|S2) ≠ D(S2|S1)

    and

    
    
    Principle

    Asymmetry
⇒
Orientation

    The current framework lacks an explicit mathematical bridge between these levels.

    This bridge appears to be a missing component of the operator hierarchy.

    

    
    
    
    5. Asymmetry as Generative Principle

    A candidate interpretation emerged:

    Symmetry
→ no intrinsic orientation selection

Asymmetry
→ orientation selection

    Under this interpretation:

    D(S1|S2) ≠ D(S2|S1)

    is not merely descriptive.

    Instead it becomes a source of admissible preference.

    The proposed hierarchy becomes:

    Distinction
↓
Asymmetry
↓
Orientation
↓
Admissibility
↓
Continuation

    

    
    
    6. Candidate Bridge Problem

    A formal mechanism is required to move from:

    D(S1|S2) ≠ D(S2|S1)

    to:

    -(i)

    Current status:

    Distinction
      ?
Orientation

    The bridge operator remains undefined.

    Potential future induction target:

    D(S1|S2) ≠ D(S2|S1)
⇒_?
-(i)

    where:

    ⇒_?

    represents an asymmetry-induced orientation operator.

    

    
    
    7. Floor Interaction

    The asymmetry-ratio discussion naturally connected to the floor concept.

    Assume:

    D(S1|S2) ≥ ε

D(S2|S1) ≥ ε

    where:

    ε > 0

    is the minimum distinguishability floor.

    Without a floor:

    D(S2|S1) = 0

    permits:

    Ω_a → ∞

    producing singular orientation behavior.

    With a floor:

    D ≥ ε

    the ratio remains bounded.

    Proposed consequence:

    ε
→ bounds D
→ bounds Ω_a
→ bounds orientation geometry

    This suggests a possible role for the floor as a regulator of orientation selection.

    

    
    
    8. Orientation Sequence Refinement

    The discussion also clarified that:

    -(i)+1

    should not be interpreted as temporal succession.

    Instead:

    -(i)+1

    means:

    next admissible orientation reference.

    Thus:

    D(S1|S2)@-(i₀)
→_a
D(S1|S2)@-(i₁)
→_a
D(S1|S2)@-(i₂)
...

    represents an admissible orientation chain rather than a time chain.

    

    
    
    9. Current Assessment

    Established:

    Ω_a =
D(S1|S2)
/ D(S2|S1)

    is a valid asymmetry metric.

    Supported:

    Ω_a = 1

    corresponds to directional symmetry.

    Hypothesized:

    Ω_a
→
orientation selection

    Unresolved:

    D(S1|S2) ≠ D(S2|S1)
⇒
-(i)

    The operator that performs this induction remains an open problem.

    

    
    
    10. Candidate Research Direction

    The most promising outcome of this discussion is not the ratio itself.

    The important possibility is:

    Distinction
→
Asymmetry
→
Orientation
→
Admissibility
→
Continuation

    If formalized, this would provide the first explicit mathematical pathway connecting relational distinction to orientation selection within the Mono-Process framework.

    End of Note.
