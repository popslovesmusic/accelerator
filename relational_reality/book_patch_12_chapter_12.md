### 12.1 From Adaptive Routing to Recursive Persistence

An adaptive routing system can be described as an arbitration procedure over a set of competing continuation signatures under residue-conditioned admissibility. The core dynamic is:
$$\Delta_{sig} \rightarrow Selection_{route}$$
Selection is not free choice; it is admissibility-gated continuation under $A$, conditioned by history $R$, and limited by reachability in the orientation array $I$.

The additional step needed for recursive identity persistence is **continuation-preserving arbitration**: routing arbitration dynamics that reinforce not only locally admissible routes, but routes that preserve the future admissibility of routing itself. This is a closure-like requirement on routing:
- if a route selection event systematically destroys the future scope of routing admissibility, the organization fragments or collapses;
- if a route selection event systematically preserves (or reinforces) the future routing scope, the organization stabilizes as a persistence basin.

We capture this chapter’s derivation target as:
$$A_{route} \rightarrow Identity_{recursive}$$
That is: when route-level admissibility is recursively preserved by the routing process itself, recursive identity persistence becomes an admissible stabilized regime.

Preferred narrative compression (this chapter): **continuation-preserving arbitration**.

### 12.3 Scope Persistence and Organizational Closure

Signal-scope organization (Chapter 11) introduced a scope boundary: the admissibility-limited set of continuation signatures coupled into routing arbitration at a locus. This chapter introduces **scope persistence** as the persistence of that arbitration boundary through time_app signatures (i.e., through stabilized ordering $\prec$), without treating the scope as a container-self metaphor.

We represent the minimal relation as:
$$R_t \rightarrow Persistence_{scope}$$
This is a procedural statement: when residue-history stabilizes, the routing scope boundary can persist as an organizational feature. The “scope” is not a spatial boundary; it is an admissibility boundary: which candidates remain reachable and legal under $A$ and $I$ given the accumulated $R$.

Scope persistence becomes organizational closure when:
- admissible re-entry remains available (repeated legal return),
- residue continuity remains coherent (no fracture of routing signature),
- selection remains aligned with reachability constraints rather than forcing repeated inadmissible candidates.

In short: closure is not enclosure. Closure is recurrence stabilization under admissibility.

### 12.4 Self-Stabilization

**Self-stabilization** is the regime in which a continuation-routing organization recursively reinforces the conditions required for its own continuation. This is not teleology and not “survival” language. It is an admissibility consequence:
- if routing arbitration dynamics repeatedly reinforce trajectories that maintain admissibility, the organization persists;
- if routing arbitration dynamics repeatedly reinforce trajectories that erode admissibility, the organization drifts, fragments, or collapses.

Self-stabilization is therefore a specific form of **recursive admissibility reinforcement**: the routing system’s selection dynamics bias toward maintaining (and sometimes deepening) the future legality of routing under $A_{route}$.

This is the operational meaning of the formal anchor:
$$A_{route} \rightarrow Identity_{recursive}$$
Identity_{recursive} is not asserted as a new ontology class; it is the stabilized organizational persistence basin that emerges when $A_{route}$ is maintained by continuation-preserving arbitration.

Preferred narrative compression (this chapter): **admissibility consequence**.

Within this framework, an organizational persistence basin denotes a recursively stabilized admissibility region supporting repeated residue-coherent re-entry under routing arbitration.

In this language, an **identity corridor** is not a “path of a self through space_app.” It is a stable, re-enterable route family in the admissibility manifold, stabilized by residue-conditioned reinforcement and orientation-weighted reachability ($I$).

### 12.8 Transition to Collapse, Failure, and Identity Drift

Recursive identity is not guaranteed. Because routing is residue-conditioned and ordering is non-commutative, the same organization can enter regimes where admissibility collapses or fragments. The failure modes of recursive identity are admissibility-based:
- scope instability: the routing scope boundary fails to persist,
- residue fracture: signature continuity breaks (constraint persistence structure fragments),
- corridor misalignment: reinforced routes stop supporting admissible re-entry,
- drift escalation: selection dynamics cease to preserve $A_{route}$.

Identity drift therefore emerges when recursive admissibility reinforcement fails to preserve residue-coherent re-entry across routing arbitration cycles.

The next chapter (Collapse, Identity Drift, and Procedural Failure) will treat drift, fragmentation, and collapse as consequences of failed recursive admissibility reinforcement: when continuation-preserving arbitration fails, identity corridors dissolve and persistence basins destabilize.
