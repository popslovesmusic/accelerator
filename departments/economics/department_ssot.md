# Economics Department Single Source of Truth (SSOT)

This document is the local SSOT for the Economics Department (`economics_app` / `procedural_economics_app`).

It governs economics-domain interpretation, derived vocabulary, provisional models, validation status, and department-local claim boundaries for economics-facing work derived from the Mono-Process Framework.

The Economics Department is subordinate to the global core and the Mathematics Department. It does not define RT/Core, primitive operators, formal admissibility, formal residue, or theorem status. It interprets selected formal structures as economics-domain projections through explicit derivation and correspondence rules.

The detailed economics domain registry remains in `docs/economics/ssot/procedural_economics_ssot.md`. This department SSOT is the peer-department governance surface for work under `departments/economics/`.

---

## Department Charter

The Economics Department exists to study how mismatch populations, organization, and admissible progression project into economics-facing observables without introducing substance-based primitives.

Boundary rule:

Mathematics defines the relational calculus. Economics interprets selected formal structures as possible descriptions of expenditure, scarcity, cost, value, wealth, and economic structure through explicit derivation and correspondence rules.

Methodological rule:

No economics primitive may be introduced unless it is derivable from the core ontology and remains traceable to the governed MPF lineage.

## Scope and Boundaries

### In Scope
- economics-domain interpretation,
- derived economic vocabulary,
- organization-facing and progression-facing economic models,
- admissibility-facing economic correspondences,
- validation and falsification status for economics-facing models,
- comparison to legacy economic vocabularies under explicit scope.

### Out of Scope
- redefining RT/Core,
- redefining `<*>_x`,
- redefining `D(a|b)`,
- redefining admissibility as a formal operator condition,
- redefining residue as a formal trace condition,
- introducing money, markets, capital, labor, or resources as primitives,
- promoting economics-domain projections into global ontology,
- treating domain projection as semantic equivalence to non-economic domains.

### Claim Ceiling
Unless separately validated, Economics Department outputs are capped at:
- `C1_model_relative` for derivation scaffolds,
- `C3_structural_comparison` for economics-domain interpretations and comparisons,
- `C2_simulation_observed` only when a specific governed economics-facing model has recoverable evidence.

No economics-facing statement may treat a projected economic category as primitive, or present a local economic interpretation as a universal proof.

## Economics Interpretation Framework

All Economics Department outputs must distinguish:
1. formal source term,
2. derivation or correspondence rule,
3. interpretation claim class,
4. validation status.

Interpretive boilerplate:

`Within the Economics Department interpretation...`

### Governed Domain-Dominant Reading

Within this department, `economics_app` remains constructively traceable to the MPF primitive lineage

`(*|*) -> (A|E) -> <*>_x -> <->_x`

while retaining a domain-dominant semantic reading. `A|E` is treated as a whole expression within the dominant organizational domain rather than as separable primitive parts. Traceability does not imply equivalence.

### Structural Economic Reading

Within this department:
- expenditure = a progression has occurred,
- scarcity = future admissible progression has contracted,
- cost = the effective consequence of that contraction,
- value = preservation, expansion, or stabilization of admissible progression,
- wealth = durable organizational capacity rather than static inventory,
- economic structure = accumulated residue of `A|E` comparison through progression.

### Non-Primitive Economic Terms

Within this department:
- money is not primitive,
- markets are not primitive,
- capital is not primitive,
- labor is not primitive,
- resources are not primitive.

These remain derived terms or projected nouns of underlying process structures.
