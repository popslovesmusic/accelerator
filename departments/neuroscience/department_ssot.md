# Neuroscience Department Single Source of Truth (SSOT)

This document is the local SSOT for the Neuroscience Department (`neuroscience_app`).

It governs neuroscience-domain interpretation, derived vocabulary, provisional models, validation status, and department-local claim boundaries for neuroscience-facing work derived from the Mono-Process Framework.

The Neuroscience Department is subordinate to the global core and the Mathematics Department. It does not define RT/Core, primitive operators, formal admissibility, formal residue, or theorem status. It interprets selected formal structures as neuroscience-domain projections through explicit derivation and correspondence rules.

---

## Department Charter

The Neuroscience Department exists to study how neural and cognitive organization can be represented as relational arrays of A|E conditions and orientation fields without introducing primitive neural ontology.

Boundary rule:

Mathematics defines the relational calculus. Neuroscience interprets selected formal structures as possible descriptions of neural organization, perception, memory, and cognition through explicit derivation and correspondence rules.

Methodological rule:

No neuroscience primitive may be introduced unless it is derivable from the core ontology and remains traceable to the governed MPF lineage.

---

## Scope and Boundaries

### In Scope
- neuroscience-domain interpretation,
- neural relational arrays,
- orientation-field modeling,
- phase-mismatch comparison,
- coupling and decoupling behavior,
- cognitive basin and stability descriptions,
- validation and falsification status for neuroscience-facing models,
- comparison to legacy neuroscience vocabularies under explicit scope.

### Out of Scope
- redefining RT/Core,
- redefining `<*>_x`,
- redefining `D(a|b)`,
- redefining admissibility as a formal operator condition,
- redefining residue as a formal trace condition,
- introducing neurons, brains, minds, or consciousness as primitives,
- promoting neuroscience-domain projections into global ontology,
- treating domain projection as semantic equivalence to non-neuroscience domains.

### Claim Ceiling
Unless separately validated, Neuroscience Department outputs are capped at:
- `C1_model_relative` for derivation scaffolds,
- `C3_structural_comparison` for neuroscience-domain interpretations and comparisons,
- `C2_simulation_observed` only when a specific governed neuroscience-facing model has recoverable evidence.

No neuroscience-facing statement may treat a projected neural category as primitive, or present a local neuroscience interpretation as a universal proof.

## Neuroscience Interpretation Framework

All Neuroscience Department outputs must distinguish:
1. formal source term,
2. derivation or correspondence rule,
3. interpretation claim class,
4. validation status.

Interpretive boilerplate:

`Within the Neuroscience Department interpretation...`

### Relational Array Reading

Within this department, a cognitive subsystem may be represented as an organized array of A|E conditions.

The whole orientation pattern of that array may be treated as an RT snapshot, and phase mismatch between snapshots may be used as the comparison quantity that drives coupling or decoupling.

### Orientation Reading

Within this department, orientation is a relational state that can be visualized with arrows but is not required to be literal spatial direction.

The orientation pattern is the state; the snapshot is the RT; stable pattern is the basin-like structure.

## Prohibited Promotions

The following are blocked:
- claiming the framework proves neural mechanism from internal structure alone,
- treating orientation arrows as literal geometry by default,
- promoting local neuroscience interpretation to universal truth,
- treating neuroscience as a replacement foundation for the core ontology,
- claiming theorem status for neuroscience derivations without formal support,
- importing independent neural primitives that are not licensed by the core ontology.
