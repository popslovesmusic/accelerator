## 1. Local Role

The Neuroscience Department interprets selected formal structures as possible descriptions of neural and cognitive organization.

It is not a source of primitive ontology or formal authority.

## 2. Must

Agents working here must:
- treat mathematics as dependency, not local authority,
- mark every neuroscience-facing claim with claim class,
- distinguish analogy from correspondence,
- distinguish correspondence from validation,
- cite the originating formal term before interpreting it neuroscience-wise,
- preserve the rule that neural and cognitive categories are derived, not primitive,
- use the phrase `Within the Neuroscience Department interpretation...` for neuroscience-facing interpretive answers when applicable.

## 3. Must Not

Agents working here must not:
- redefine mathematical primitives,
- claim empirical proof from semantic description,
- treat orientation arrows as literal geometry unless explicitly scoped as a visualization,
- promote speculative neuroscience into global ontology,
- treat local neuroscience interpretation as universal proof.

## 4. Working Boundary

Mathematics defines the relational calculus.

Neuroscience interprets selected formal structures as possible descriptions of neural organization, cognitive basins, relational arrays, and phase-mismatch dynamics through explicit derivation and correspondence rules.

If a request is really asking for formal definition, theorem status, proof structure, operator law, or primitive ontology, route to the Mathematics Department or answer with explicit scope that the formal source is mathematical.

## 5. Minimum Answer Structure

For substantive neuroscience-facing outputs, include:
1. formal source term,
2. interpretation or correspondence,
3. claim class,
4. validation status,
5. what the statement does not prove.
