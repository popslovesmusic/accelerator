# Notebook 15 Design: Articulation and Continuation Geometry

## Scope

Notebook 15 moves away from machine-learning classification and toward structural articulation analysis.

Current status: `C1_SPECIFICATION_ONLY`.

## Progression Context

- Notebook 11: outcome-proximal descriptors.
- Notebook 12: mechanism isolation.
- Notebook 13: topology captures the bounded geometry.
- Notebook 14: generic topology retains strong predictive power after near-definitional features are removed, with articulation hierarchy emerging as the strongest individual feature family tested.

Notebook 15 asks whether articulation can be linked mathematically to continuation geometry.

## Questions

- Why do articulation points matter?
- Does every obstruction require an articulation hierarchy?
- Is there a minimum articulation depth associated with obstruction?
- Can articulation structure be characterized as a theorem rather than a predictive feature?
- Are articulation points causal, or are they consequences of a deeper invariant?

## Method

Notebook 15 does not use classifier accuracy as the primary method.

It builds theorem-candidate tables:

- candidate statement,
- logical direction,
- required definitions,
- observed support in bounded domain,
- counterexample count,
- example counterexample config IDs,
- proof obligations,
- permitted interpretation.

## Candidate Families

- Obstruction requires articulation.
- Obstruction implies articulation depth bounded above or below.
- Universal reconvergence excludes certain articulation hierarchy forms.
- Partial reconvergence corresponds to mixed articulation separation.
- Articulation is consequence of a deeper closure-lattice or basin-decomposition invariant.

## Non-Claims

Notebook 15 does not prove a theorem. It does not establish causality. It does not validate external physical systems. It can only create bounded theorem candidates or counterexamples until formal mathematics work is added.
