## Submitted proposal: contextual RT roles

The proposal reduces the ontology to one identity: `RT`. Aspect, Composite, and Primitive are not separate kinds of entities; they are contextual observational roles of the same RT under a current working proposition.

The proposition defines the working domain. An RT such as `RT_oh` can be decomposed into child RTs such as `RT_n`, `RT_i`, `RT_e`, `RT_m`, `RT_c`, and `RT_s`, where those labels are aspect roles relative to the current proposition. Each child remains an RT and may become the next proposition.

Primitive ground is contextual. An RT becomes primitive when, under the current proposition, further decomposition would destroy the identity or no longer be necessary to decide the proposition. A primitive in one domain may therefore become an aspect RT in another domain. Identity remains invariant while role changes with recursive context.

## Recursive evaluation and closure

The proposed cycle is:

```text
choose proposition
  -> recursively decompose RT into aspect RTs
  -> stop at the first closed RT sufficient for the proposition
  -> evaluate the contextual primitive
  -> resolve an orientation field
  -> reconstruct by recursive closure
  -> continue upward to the root RT
```

Evaluation occurs only at contextual primitive ground. The proposed outcomes `+` and `-` are placeholders for orientation-field results, not scalar values. They indicate whether the evaluated RT generates or excludes the current proposition, but their internal representation is an ordered field/array rather than arithmetic magnitude. Scalar or angular estimates are downstream observables only.

Closure is the reconstruction operation. Primitive results reconstruct into aspect RTs and then into the parent RT, with each level receiving an orientation derived from the orientations beneath it. The same closure operator is proposed at each recursive level.

## Hexahedron and sphere observation model

The `12 x 12` ordered array is proposed as one face of a finite hexahedron. The hexahedron is described as finite in size but potentially infinite in recursive capacity. The observer does not necessarily resolve all recursive depth; it stops at the first closed RT sufficient for the current proposition.

An outer sphere is proposed as the causal admissibility window and observer interaction boundary. Orientation markings define possible boundary positions. A start marker `A` and stop marker `B` define a trajectory, and a geodesic is proposed to carry the topology/ordered sequence of the observed transition. The orientation determines the observed slice of the hexahedron face.

The hexahedra do not directly interact. Observation is instead defined as a boundary interaction: a distinct causal perturbation, analogized as a pressure wave, propagates through admissibility and changes the orientation and observed slice. This analogy is not a physical claim.

## Distinction density, decoupling, and reservoir hypothesis

Distinction density is proposed as the symmetry reference. A causal perturbation redistributes density and changes orientation and slice; it does not increase density. The stronger conservation statement is scoped to closure: density is conserved under closure, while decoupling can remove density from the current domain.

When a component reaches `0 dof`, it becomes non-admissible and inert relative to the current domain. Its density is not destroyed but decouples from the current causal window. The proposal describes the resulting latent background as an entropy-like reservoir relative to composition and decomposition.

MTO/OTM is proposed as the cycle for this reservoir:

- many-to-one decomposition can collapse multiple decoupled structures into latent undifferentiated capacity;
- one-to-many composition can differentiate latent capacity into new structural possibilities.

This is a conceptual hypothesis. No thermodynamic identity, conservation theorem, physical wave model, or empirical result is claimed.
